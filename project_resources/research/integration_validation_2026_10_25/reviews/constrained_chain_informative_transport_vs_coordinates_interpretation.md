These results answer different integration questions. The gap measures state correspondence of the imposed RNA-anchored, global one-to-one Euclidean assignment, relative to a finite-pool squared-state oracle and random expectation. scMRDR has the smaller gap here. That supports this assignment's state correspondence; it does not establish interchangeable shared coordinates, observed paired-cell recovery, or a correct joint biological coupling. A bijection uses every target once by construction and can therefore conceal concentration in unconstrained neighbor searches.

The frozen decoder fits source training cells with `StandardScaler + Ridge(alpha=1)` and transfers both unchanged to target embeddings. scMRDR's RNA→H3K27ac/H3K4me3 mean R² values are -3.4083/-3.5127 in seed 0 and -10.1675/-12.6399 in seed 1. These are failures of that declared source calibration relative to the target-mean benchmark. In seed 0, the first state-coordinate scores remain positive (0.5140/0.2159), while the second is strongly negative (-7.3306/-7.2414); in seed 1 both coordinates are negative in both directions. All four remaining direction means are positive in each seed. Avoid generalizing this directional failure to all coordinates, all readouts, or loss of all biological information. Earlier exploratory probe success on another world does not establish the mechanism here.

Coverage counts target cells receiving at least one directed k-neighbor edge; it is not correspondence accuracy. The seed-0 minimum is RNA→H3K4me3; seed-1 minima are both mark→RNA searches, while RNA→H3K4me3 coverage is 0.36021. Thus native neighbor use is concentrated despite the forced assignment's low state gap. scGLUE has stronger weakest-direction decoder transfer and broader minimum coverage here, alongside its larger assignment gap. Keep those properties visible separately and retain the frozen selection rule. Neither positive transfer nor broad coverage certifies integration.

A concise analytic example would help explain compatibility without diagnosing these fits. Let source embedding be `(S,0)` and target embedding `(3T,c)`, with independent unpaired state samples S,T uniform on [-1,1] and c>0. Strictly convex Euclidean assignment cost favors sorted-rank matching, as does the squared-state oracle, so their finite-pool assignment permutations coincide and G=0. Yet the ideal source decoder d(x,y)=x predicts 3T on target cells and has population R²=-3. Native nearest neighbors prefer target states around S/3. This illustrative one-dimensional construction is not an exact finite-sample reproduction of the fixed Ridge pipeline, an estimate of these fits' scales, or a prediction of their coverage fractions.

The existing covariance bound is compatible with this discrepancy: it controls smooth functions of true state under the actual assignment using absolute transport cost. It does not control an assay-trained latent decoder applied off its calibration support. It also does not establish residual covariance, conditional independence, or causal recovery. The original [GLUE study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-022-01284-4) and [scMRDR study](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24987v2) motivate alignment and preservation checks; their objectives do not make the present diagnostics equivalent.
