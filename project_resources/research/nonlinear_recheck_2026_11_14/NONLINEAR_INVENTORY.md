# Nonlinear identification: bounded recheck, 2026-11-14

**No: there were not only two nonlinear results.** The 2025 and 2026 content/style papers were the two *newly flagged papers with the same literal variability-premise issue*. The earlier program had reviewed several other nonlinear results. This recheck also found meaningful theorem-level coverage gaps in Xie, Kong, Yan and Nguyen–Fu; the prior broad literature ledger's passing citations were not a substitute for examining these theorem statements. ## How to interpret the unresolved issues

The literal A4/3.2/3.3 issue is a property of the printed assumptions in four specific papers: a null-set change creates an exact non-cylinder while leaving every atomless probability unchanged. It does **not** show that all nonlinear identification theorems are wrong or that no suitable corrected condition can work. It means a repaired condition and proof need separate verification; no such repair is supplied here. The more general practical obstacles—unknown/noisy measurement kernels, different feature spaces, absent paired cells or suitable observed auxiliary variables, unverified domain variation, selection mismatch, and assumptions about latent geometry—apply in varying combinations and should not be collapsed into that logical defect.

No newly inspected result establishes recovery of the original genomic feature-level causal graph from the permitted unpaired count laws. The precise status of every model class outside this bounded sample remains open.
