# Skeptical review of two nonlinear content–style papers

Scope: primary-paper assumption and proof check, 2026-11-14. This is not an exhaustive literature count or a test of trained models. Versions: Shrestha & Fu arXiv:2411.03755v3; Timilsina et al. arXiv:2605.17827v1. HTML line numbers below are source locators, not printed page numbers.

## Literal variability assumption

Both [Shrestha & Fu, Assumption 3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.03755#S3.SS1) (HTML lines 126–130) and [Timilsina et al., Assumption 3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17827#S3.SS2) (lines 133–137) quantify over **any measurable** A with positive probability under every domain, excluding only exact sets B × S, and demand that some pair of domain probabilities differ. Their latent spaces are continuous open subsets (respectively lines 89–93 and 80–84); the papers use densities, and Shrestha–Fu Theorem 3.4 explicitly requires positive finite joint densities on all of C × S (lines 148–150). For atomless distributions and a nontrivial style space choose z0=(c0,s0) and A=(C × S)\{z0}. Every domain assigns A probability one, while A is not exactly B × S: its c0 fiber is S\{s0}, neither empty nor all of S. Thus the stated condition cannot hold in that continuous atomless setting. There is no exclusion of common-null modifications in the statements. This is a literal assumption defect, not a counterexample to an implication conditional on an impossible premise, and not evidence that a trained model fails.

The prior literature citation and probabilistic context make an almost-everywhere interpretation plausible, but neither quoted assumption states it. The [2025 paper Appendix B.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.03755) lines 510–513 reproduces the same exact-set form for earlier work; this alone does not establish the original authors' intended convention. A common-null-set cylinder equivalence would remove this particular singleton objection, but it is a proposed revision, not a verified repair of the theorems.

## Proof issues distinct from the singleton

* **2025 content:** [Appendix C.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.03755#A3.SS1), lines 543–580, uses matching of learned content distributions to argue that preimages are exact cylinders. At lines 573–574 it asserts a nonempty non-cylinder remainder F has strictly positive measure; nonemptiness does not imply that. The assumption also requires positive probability under *every* domain, which the cited step does not establish. An a.e. formulation would need an a.e. proof and a justified passage to the stated pointwise conclusion; it is not automatic. At line 594 the stated inference that a nonzero Jacobian determinant implies global injectivity is invalid in general (it gives local invertibility); global injectivity may be provable by another argument, but this step alone does not show it.
* **2025 style:** [Appendix C.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.03755#A3.SS2), lines 598–606, concludes functional nondependence of learned style on c from statistical independence. That inference is false by itself: let c be uniform on (0,1), s uniform on the unit disk in R² independently, and define learned style R(2πc)s. This is smooth and invertible jointly with c, and learned style is independent of c by rotational invariance, yet depends on c. This example challenges the inference at that step; it does not satisfy the paper's full domain-variability assumption and is not a complete counterexample to its theorem. The unknown-dimension proof explicitly reuses this style argument (Appendix D lines 644–647). Appendix D line 672 also asserts differentiability implies continuity of the derivative, which requires a stronger regularity condition.
* **2026 content and style:** [Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17827#A2), lines 417–428, imports the 2025 content proof, so its content claim inherits the unresolved variability and content-proof questions. Its subsequent orthogonality projection argument (lines 441–477) is a distinct conditional style argument once content identification, the relevant differentiability, rank and learned orthogonality are granted; the singleton observation alone does not refute that algebra. Line 452 states rank(AB)=min(rank A, rank B) generally, which is not a general matrix identity; here full rank of the product and the dS column/row dimensions suffice for the desired full-rank factors. Lines 479–482 require care in deriving a single global invertible style map across the union of domains; local zero derivatives/full rank alone are not a general proof of global injectivity.
* **2026 approximate orthogonality:** [Theorem 3.6 and Appendix C](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17827#S3.SS3), lines 154–179 and 489–529, give a derivative leakage bound, not full style identification. The displayed theorem states approximate CSDI and style rank, whereas the proof reuses content identification, learned-decoder exact orthogonality, and the equality of style tangent spaces from Theorem 3.4. Those dependencies need to be explicit for a self-contained reading. The bound also worsens when the smallest learned style-Jacobian singular value is small; it is a local derivative statement, not automatically a uniform global error bound.

## Interpretation

These papers publish mathematical identification claims under structural and exact population constraints; the concerns above are about the assumptions and particular proof steps. They do not establish universal nonidentifiability, an algorithmic failure, or the impossibility of a corrected theorem. The 2025 unknown-dimension result further relies on overestimated dimensions, exact optimization and sparsity; the 2026 paper trades statistical independence for orthogonal tangent subspaces and assumes an invertible model with matched distributions. Neither paper alone establishes applicability to a given genomics setting.

## Follow-up: almost-everywhere exclusion alone also fails for finite independent-block domains

The stronger objection is valid under a finite number N of atomless style laws and content–style independence in each domain, as in Shrestha–Fu Assumption 3.1. Extend each style probability measure μ_n to the common measurable space S by zero outside its domain support. Define the finite vector measure μ(H)=(μ_1(H),…,μ_N(H)). Each component is atomless, so the vector measure is atomless. By [Lyapunov's convexity theorem](https://www.econ2.jhu.edu/REPEC/papers/wp586_khan.pdf), Theorem 2 and Lemma 2, its range is convex. Both (0,…,0) and (1,…,1) are in the range; hence their midpoint is in the range. There is a measurable H ⊂ S such that μ_n(H)=1/2 for every n. This result is existence of a measurable set, not necessarily an interval or another restricted class of sets.

Set A=C×H. Under the independent-block model, P_n(A)=1/2 in every domain. For every measurable content set B, independence also gives P_n(A Δ (B×S)) = P_c(B)(1/2) + (1−P_c(B))(1/2) = 1/2 in every domain. Thus A is not a content cylinder even modulo each domain's null sets, yet violates the proposed variability condition. Replacing exact set equality by equality almost everywhere alone therefore cannot make this unrestricted all-measurable condition satisfiable in the finite-domain, atomless-style, block-independent class. This strengthens the conclusion for the 2025 block-independent setting and for any 2026 model within that class; it does not establish impossibility for every dependent model, an infinite collection of domains, a restricted family of test sets, or nonlinear identification under other assumptions. It is not an algorithmic or unconditional theorem counterexample.
