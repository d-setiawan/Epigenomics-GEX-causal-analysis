# Independent scoped check of DICA finite-sample premise

Astra, 2026-11-15. Primary [DICA Theorem 3.3, condition 5, equation (11)](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17040) was inspected independently of the literature worker. For every parameter pair, the displayed premise places a strictly positive lower Lipschitz bound on differences of a scalar volume surrogate; the upper bound implies continuity. The statement does not provide a restricted parameter-domain construction that resolves the issue below. This review concerns that premise, not the population Theorem 3.2.

Fix one latent point s. Suppose the admitted parameter set contains a two-dimensional closed disk of positive radius (in particular, any open subset of Euclidean parameter space of dimension at least two contains one). Parameterize its boundary by theta(t)=c+r(cos(t)e1+sin(t)e2). Write v(t)=V(s;theta(t)) and h(t)=v(t)-v(t+pi). Continuity gives continuous h, and periodicity gives h(pi)=-h(0). If h(0)=0 there is already an antipodal equal-value pair; otherwise the intermediate value theorem yields t in (0,pi) with h(t)=0. The two parameter points are distinct, separated by distance 2r, but their scalar surrogate values agree. This contradicts any positive lower Lipschitz constant. This is a deterministic mathematical argument, not a generated benchmark or inference from experimental failure.

Thus the literal condition cannot hold on such a parameter domain. Parameter symmetries furnish another obstruction whenever two different admitted parameter vectors represent the same surrogate. A finite, one-dimensional or suitably restricted domain could evade the disk argument, so this is not a proof that every conceivable class satisfying Theorem 3.3 is empty. Its application to ordinary multi-parameter neural function classes needs an explicit restriction or a substantially different stability condition. We do not claim the theorem conclusion is false without its premise, invalidate the independent population theorem, or infer count-noise robustness from this review. The current implementation's regularized finite optimization is a separate estimator and does not certify the displayed premise.
