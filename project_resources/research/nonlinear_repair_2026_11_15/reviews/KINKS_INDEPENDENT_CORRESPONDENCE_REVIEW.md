## The literal theorem and counterexample

I checked [Manring–Huang, arXiv:2609.21926v1, Assumption 2 and Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.21926v1), especially §4 and Appendix A. The source assumes a connected-support scalar density with finitely many analytic log-density pieces, nonzero jumps in the first derivative at each breakpoint, nonempty intervening intervals, and the specified central analytic continuation condition. Its theorem quantifies over a real-analytic diffeomorphism preserving the iid source law and claims signed-permutation/translation form.

For `0<a<1`, `F(x)=x+a log(cosh x)` is real analytic with `F'(x)=1+a tanh(x)` strictly positive and bounded away from zero; it maps R bijectively to R and has analytic inverse. The proposed `ρ(x)=½ exp(−|F(x)|)F'(x)` is exactly the pullback of standard Laplace, so it is positive, normalized and supported on R. F is increasing with unique zero at 0. The two log-density pieces `F+log F'−log 2` and `−F+log F'−log 2` are real analytic through 0; their derivative values are `a+1` and `a−1`, with jump −2. There is one breakpoint, both intervals are nonempty, and the odd-breakpoint continuation condition holds. These checks cover all three parts of the stated Assumption 2.

The involution `h=F⁻¹∘(−F)` is analytic, globally invertible and preserves ρ. From `F(h(x))=−F(x)`, `h(0)=0`, `h'(0)=−1` and `h''(0)=−2a≠0`. It is therefore not an affine signed permutation plus translation. Applying the same map to each iid coordinate preserves the iid product distribution in any finite dimension. It gives an actual counterexample to the theorem's **signed-affine** conclusion for its written general prior class. It is a coordinatewise transformation, however, so it **does not refute identifiability up to arbitrary componentwise invertible transformations and permutation**. It does not refute the exact standard-Laplace special case, the behavior of a trained estimator, or unrelated model classes.

The source appendix's orientation-reversing reduction deserves the stated criticism. In this example `h(x)=x r(x)` with analytic `r(0)=−1` and `r'(0)=−a`; nevertheless `logρ(h(x))−logρ(x)=log F'(h(x))−log F'(x)` is globally analytic. Thus this is more than a speculative proof gap: the purported reversal conclusion fails for an admissible density.

## Exact iid standard-Laplace proposition

The density change-of-variables equation holds almost everywhere and extends everywhere by continuity, since both densities and a diffeomorphism's Jacobian are continuous and the Laplace density is everywhere positive. Its logarithmic Jacobian is real analytic: a nowhere-zero analytic determinant has locally constant sign, and the log of its absolute value is locally analytic. Hence `Σ|h_j(x)|−Σ|x_i|` is analytic.

For a fixed input hyperplane `P_i`, first take points off all other input coordinate hyperplanes. If no output component vanished there, the output absolute-value sum would be locally analytic while `|x_i|` has an uncancelled transverse kink. Therefore the finitely many output zero sets cover that relatively open subset of `P_i`. On the connected hyperplane, a nonzero restricted real-analytic function has zero set of empty interior; a finite union of these closed nowhere-dense sets cannot cover an open set. Some output `h_j` vanishes on an open patch and hence on `P_i`. Two distinct outputs cannot both vanish identically on `P_i`, because their Jacobian rows would both be normal to `P_i`; the invertible Jacobian would then have dependent rows. Removing the other finitely many proper analytic zero sets yields a patch where exactly one output vanishes and all other input/output signs are fixed.

Tangential derivatives of `h_j` vanish on the hyperplane, and invertibility forces its normal derivative nonzero. On crossing it the sign of `h_j` changes. The two analytic expressions for the same analytic difference agree by the real-analytic identity theorem, so their difference `2(σ h_j−x_i)` vanishes on an open neighborhood. Thus `h_j=σ x_i` there and, by analytic continuation on connected R^k, everywhere. Distinct input coordinates cannot select the same output; k inputs and k outputs force a signed permutation. For k=1 the hyperplane is a point and the same two-side analytic argument applies separately. Translation is excluded by the density equation and the unique kink location. I found no blocking gap in the Baire/patch/continuation argument under the stated **global** analytic diffeomorphism assumptions.

## Count composition

Full product-Poisson count-law equality identifies the entire nonnegative intensity mixing law using the separately reviewed full-real-box Laplace-transform argument. Applying the known componentwise log to strictly positive rates gives equality of the noiseless `f(S)` and `g(S)` laws. For square global real-analytic diffeomorphisms f and g, `g⁻¹∘f` is an analytic Laplace-law-preserving self-map; the proposition therefore yields the signed-permutation equivalence. This is a population, within-assay result. It neither recovers the rate/source of an individual noisy cell exactly nor aligns independent signed-permutation ambiguities across unpaired assays. Conditional posterior statements, cross-assay coupling and causal effects require their own assumptions. It does not formalize the more general undercomplete/additive-noise settings of the source paper.

**Required wording:** keep the signed-affine versus weaker componentwise distinction explicit in any synthesis of the counterexample; the original note's scope section should add that sentence. The analytic full-box and Poisson conditional-independence assumptions must accompany the positive count claim. No mathematical correction to the proposed restricted-Laplace proof is required on this review.
