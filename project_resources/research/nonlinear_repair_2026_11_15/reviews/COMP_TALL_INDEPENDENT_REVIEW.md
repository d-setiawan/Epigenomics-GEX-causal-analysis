# Independent review of CoMP unequal-width corollary E2

2026-11-15. Verdict: **accept as a conditional population corollary** of the previously reviewed square ICA transport E and product-Poisson mixing-law injection. This does not establish the assumptions for S1 or provide a finite-sample estimator. Neither this review nor the corollary changes an experiment or constitutes a Lean proof.

For assay c, the joint count law identifies the rate mixing measure and hence, because rates are positive and log is a measurable bijection, the log-rate law. Unit variances and independence give Cov(S)=I, regardless of the mean of S; therefore Cov(log R_c)=M_c M_cᵀ. A full-column-rank M_c makes this covariance rank d with column space exactly col(M_c), so d and the ambient subspace are population-identifiable. For an orthonormal basis Q_c of that subspace, A_c=Q_cᵀM_c is square and invertible; Q_cᵀ log R_c=A_c S is precisely the square model. The square theorem then gives the common-source transport A_c A_0⁻¹, which is unique under the stated independent non-Gaussian coordinates and absence of nontrivial signed-permutation symmetry.

The basis calculation is exact: replacing Q_c by Q_c O_c for orthogonal O_c changes A_c to O_cᵀA_c and reduced transport to O_cᵀ(A_c A_0⁻¹)O_0. Hence Q_c O_c [O_cᵀ(A_c A_0⁻¹)O_0] O_0ᵀQ_0ᵀ equals Q_c A_c A_0⁻¹Q_0ᵀ. This proves the same U_c on the identified source subspace; any ambient map differing by a linear map that vanishes there has the same restriction. Only the subspace-restricted map is identified from these data. Equal feature widths and like-feature semantics are unnecessary.

The exponential fixture is valid: S~Exp(1) has variance 1, is non-Gaussian and is not invariant in law under S→−S; both displayed column vectors are nonzero, so their subspace ranks are one, and the restricted map sends t(−1,−2) to t(−3,−4,−5). Each rate is in (0,1], and the construction is a nonvacuous analytic model, not a new dataset. The logarithmic rate support in that fixture is a ray inside the identified line, not the whole line; the linear model specifies its unique linear extension to the line. Arbitrary extrapolation outside the assumed model class is not a data-only conclusion.

The proof concerns *linear* log-rate maps with no assay-specific intercept. Covariance centering does not identify or remove a free intercept: extending to affine maps can reintroduce an affine reflection about a source mean even when the law has no nontrivial linear sign symmetry. Allowing different selected-population laws, unknown depth/capture, unknown non-Poisson kernel, or lack of finite source variance also leaves the proof's scope. The retrieved mixing law and its theoretical covariance need not be stably estimable from sparse finite counts; a predicted noisy target count further requires an explicit measurement coupling, and a causal interpretation requires an intervention/invariance model. C01–C03 did not fit E2.
