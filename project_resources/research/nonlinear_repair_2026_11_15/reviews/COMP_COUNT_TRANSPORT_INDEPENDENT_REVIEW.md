## Primary theorem and proof correspondence

I checked [Foster et al., CoMP, Theorem 2 and Appendix E.1, including Theorem 6 and Assumption 7](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/foster22a/foster22a.pdf), printed pp. 18–20 (PDF pp. 19–21). Their noiseless model writes `x=A_c B s`, with independent non-Gaussian unit-variance coordinates, and excludes each nonidentity signed-permutation symmetry of s. Their ICA step gives column permutation/sign ambiguity; common latent distribution across conditions and the asymmetry restriction force the same ambiguity in all conditions, so it cancels in `A_c A_0^{-1}`. This supports the corresponding linear-law identification invoked by E. It is an imported ICA result, not a kernel-checked result in this project. The source's explicit counterfactual terminology is tied to its structural model; E appropriately states a transport and does not infer a biological intervention from assay identity.

The square invertibility assumption is material: each assay coordinate space and latent space have the same finite d, and each `M_c` is nonsingular. Distinct labeled feature spaces of equal dimension are permitted mathematically: each `M_c` is a separately specified map from the common latent coordinate space to its assay's coordinate space, and `M_c M_0^{-1}` is a map between them. Nothing in the proof requires feature j in one assay to be the same gene or genomic region as feature j in another. Equal panel widths alone do not verify square mixing, a common latent law or any other model assumption; no coordinate pairing may be silently inferred from panel order.

## Count composition and uniqueness

For each assay, independent Poisson coordinates conditional on its finite positive rate vector R give a count PGF whose values on a real open box identify the entire multivariate Laplace transform and therefore `P(R)`. The separately reviewed full-box argument (`REPAIR_PROOF_REVIEW.md`) supplies this nontrivial measure-injection step; equality only on a diagonal would not. Coordinatewise log is a known measurable bijection `(0,∞)^d ↔ R^d`, so it identifies `P(L)` as a law, not the latent rate of a particular observed cell. The competing models must obey the same product-Poisson channel, positive rates, known log link and selected-population semantics; the link and channels are not learned from assay identity.

For any invertible T that maps the assay-0 log-rate law to the assay-c log-rate law, `H=M_c^{-1} T M_0` preserves the law of S. ICA uniqueness makes H a signed permutation: H maps independent non-Gaussian sources to independent non-Gaussian sources; unit variances exclude non-unit scalings. The stated absence of nonidentity signed-permutation distributional symmetries then gives H=I and uniquely fixes `T=M_c M_0^{-1}`. Thus exponentiating gives `F_c(r)=exp(T log r)` on the positive orthant. The statement is stronger than merely choosing the best fitting transport from a particular objective: within the class, no other invertible linear T maps the two identified laws. The target is unique, while source coordinates and their common signed-permutation convention remain unlabelled.

A competing representation may have a different unknown source law, but it must still have independent non-Gaussian unit-variance coordinates, nonsingular maps and **one common source law across all its own assays**. The uniqueness argument applies to its induced transport because it maps the same two log-rate laws. The absence-of-symmetry condition is already sufficient for the original source law to rule out a second transport; keeping it for every competing representation makes the model class explicit. Non-Gaussianity and asymmetry are restrictions, not established properties of the permitted counts. This proof concerns population laws; it gives neither a computational inversion method nor finite-sample consistency.

## Structural coupling and observation noise

Equal source *marginals* across separate assay populations alone do not specify a true same-cell coupling. The proposed model explicitly uses one shared S in the same-cell structural construction, so `R_c=F_c(R_0)` almost surely; under that assumption the joint rate law is the pushforward of `P(R_0)`. The distinction is substantive and should remain prominent when comparing this class to the unpaired observation design. A model could instead specify independent copies of equally distributed sources and obtain the same assay marginals with a different rate joint; that alternative lies outside E's shared-S structural class.

The within-assay product-Poisson restriction also does not fix cross-assay noise dependence. Assuming mutually independent assay measurement vectors conditional on common S in addition to each assay's product-Poisson kernel identifies the conditional same-cell count joint. Without it, separate conditional Poisson kernels can be coupled by independent uniforms or by a common uniform through their conditional quantile functions, producing the same assay marginals and generally different cross-assay count joints. This completion assumption does not identify an intervention effect, a regulatory DAG, or the original biological coupling. It also does not imply individual latent rate or same-cell cross-assay count is determined from a single noisy count vector.

## Constructive examples and limitations

For S~Exponential(1), `Var(S)=1`; it is non-Gaussian and has no sign symmetry. With M0=−1 and M1=−2, `R0=e^(−S)` is Uniform(0,1), `R1=R0²`, and `T=2`; the full count distributions are proper with all intensity moments finite. This is a valid nonvacuity example despite nonzero source mean: neither the stated E model nor this linear symmetry proof requires centering. For dimension d>1, choose independent gamma coordinates with pairwise-distinct shape parameters, and scale each by the reciprocal of its standard deviation. Their positive support excludes sign flips and distinct scaled marginal laws exclude nontrivial permutations. Invertible negative diagonal matrices give bounded strictly positive rates and a valid example. These are analytic specifications, not new generated worlds.

The practical CoMP objective and its nonlinear decoder are not established by the cited noiseless linear identification theorem to estimate this count model faithfully. Product-Poisson observation, feature-level selection and local-QC transport remain unverified for S1; library-depth proxies need not be known independent exposures. An NB bridge requires its own specified known-kernel injection proof. These restrictions are materially stronger than ordinary unpaired integration and can fail despite good predictive performance.

**Wording recommendation:** qualify “common selected-population source law” with the additional shared-S structural coupling whenever a joint-law or same-cell statement is made, and describe cross-assay conditional independence as an extra assumption, as the proposal already does. Cite the Poisson injection as a separate proved argument rather than as part of CoMP Theorem 2. No blocking error in the class E identification statement was found under the explicit assumptions.
