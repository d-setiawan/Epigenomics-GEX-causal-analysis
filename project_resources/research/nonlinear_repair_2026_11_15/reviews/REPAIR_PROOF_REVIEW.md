# Independent review of repair proposals A–D

Reviewer: independent proof reviewer, 2026-11-15; did not author `theory/REPAIR_PROPOSALS.md`. Verdict: **accept the stated population arguments with the scope and clarification conditions below**. B supplies a sufficient identification theorem for a separately specified finite product-Poisson mixture, not evidence that a particular fit or S1 satisfies it. C and D are different nonvacuous continuous classes, not restorations of the unrestricted content/style theorem. No fit, dataset or evaluator outcome was inspected in this review. ## Full multivariate Poisson injection

**Accept.** Let μ and ν be arbitrary probability measures on `[0,∞)^d`, with coordinates conditionally independent Poisson given the entire rate vector. Equality of count laws makes their PGFs equal on `(0,1)^d`, hence Laplace transforms equal on the full real box `(0,1)^d`. For fixed positive-real coordinates except the first, the difference is holomorphic in the first right half-plane and vanishes on an interval; the one-variable identity theorem extends it there. Iterate coordinate by coordinate, holding previously extended coordinates at arbitrary complex values with positive real part and remaining coordinates in the real box. Equality follows on the product of right half-planes; dominated convergence at `ε1−it`, ε↓0, gives identical characteristic functions and hence identical probability measures. Holomorphy follows from boundedness of polynomial-times-exponential factors on compact subsets of the positive half-plane, even without finite moments of μ. The equality is on a **real box**, not merely a diagonal or a sequence. This proves distributional, not pathwise, inversion; unknown capture, dependent observation noise, endogenous depth, truncation or other kernels need their own injectivity proof.

The cited [López et al. (2024)](https://web.stanford.edu/group/pritchardlab/publications/pdfs/Lopez24.pdf), Appendix D.2, Proposition 2 (printed pp. 895–896), is the relevant count reduction. The reported diagonal argument cannot by itself justify multivariate analytic equality: `z1−z2` vanishes on the diagonal but is not the zero function. This independently supplied full-box argument establishes the product-Poisson claim without deciding the paper's other cases or its broader nonlinear theorem. A visual check of the cited PDF is appropriate before alleging any further transcription or sign defect; this review does not depend on such an allegation.

## B: distinct finite rate vectors and common weights

**Accept as a population theorem.** Equality of each observed assay count law identifies its mixing measure by the preceding result. A finite probability measure has a unique list of distinct support atoms and their positive masses, so it identifies K, unordered full rate vectors and masses for each assay. If all masses are pairwise distinct and exactly common across selected assay populations, matching the mass values yields the unique common permutation and identifies the aligned component emissions and Bayes posterior for every count y of positive model probability. One feature need not separate every atom: `(1,1),(1,2),(2,1)` are distinct vectors while neither coordinate has three distinct values. The original anchor proof is a valid stronger special case.

K, positive weights, distinct vectors in **each** assay and pairwise-distinct *exactly shared* weights must be restrictions on every competing representation; otherwise zero-weight or duplicate components can be added, or repeated weights permuted. If the true selected populations have different weights due to assay-local QC, matching them is not justified. Product-Poisson emissions are strong and testable only through implications of the model; selecting a finite model does not show its latent classes are biological states. Bayesian membership is identified as a function of an observed vector; the realized state is generally uncertain when component supports overlap. Zero-rate special cases can yield some certain cells, so do not state universal uncertainty.

**Joint completion is optional and additional.** Let `F_ak` be the identified assay-a conditional count law. If one assumes the same population and *mutual* conditional independence of all assay blocks given the aligned Z, the joint is `Σ_k w_k ⊗_a F_ak`. This identifies a statistical joint under that added restriction. Without it, marginals and weights do not determine residual cross-assay couplings. For example with K=1 and a Poisson(1) law in each assay, independent Poisson draws and an identical shared Poisson draw have the same assay marginals but different joints. Within-assay coordinate independence does not imply between-assay conditional independence. No direction or intervention effect follows.

The finite fixture in the proposal has positive weights 0.2, 0.3, 0.5 and distinct rate vectors in both assays, so it is nonempty and exhibits differing feature orderings. This is an analytic specification, not validation of an estimator. **Astra may use this theorem to motivate a frozen training-only model check**; this review does not approve fitting, common-weight plausibility, architecture, finite-sample consistency or empirical model adequacy. In particular the biological selected weights and paired-holdout transport remain unverified.

## A and C: mean-contrast restrictions

**Accept A, conditional on the stated encoder restriction.** Equal candidate distributions have equal finite means; the nonzero style-mean contrast forces β=0. Strictly increasing h gives the probability-integral rank of C almost surely (interpret F_C as strictly increasing on the relevant support, not literally on all of ℝ). The supplied bounded-uniform example satisfies the conditions. The candidate class is defined relative to latent coordinates and is not generally enforceable from unpaired observations; no count recovery is implied.

**Accept C with a key observation-level distinction.** With common content law and finite means, observable contrast matrix M=BD. Full-column-rank B and full-row-rank D imply `col(M)=col(B)`. An observable left-nullspace basis W annihilates B; `[A B]` nonsingular makes WA invertible. Any W basis change is an invertible left factor, so the identified target is the population content representation up to GL(p). The explicit two-dimensional uniform construction has a nonzero contrast and invertible `[A B]`; exp followed by product-Poisson measurement supplies a nonvacuous nonlinear-link count class. No independence of C and S is needed for the mean calculation. Observations of counts identify the **law** of V through the injection lemma and known measurable inverse link; E|V|<∞ is required to identify M. They do not reveal V or WV for a particular cell. The conditional posterior law of WV given count y follows from the identified joint kernel for positive-probability y, up to the same basis change. Calling a matrix W an enforceable count-per-cell encoder without this qualification would be a blocking overclaim. The current heterogeneous assay coordinates and unavailable group labels do not provide the same-measurement domain design automatically.

## D: auxiliary-modulated nonlinear count model

**Accept the stated measure-ratio theorem with these exact conditions.** Observed U has `mk+1` fixed, common label values of positive probability; the statistic vector has dimension exactly `mk`, the competing parameterization has the same dimension, and *both* natural-parameter contrast matrices are invertible. Conditional latent laws must share a positive common-support density within each model, giving mutual absolute continuity across labels; normalizers and statistics must be finite on that support. Decoder f must be injective with measurable image and measurable inverse, and its conditional product-Poisson observation kernel must be common across labels. Equal joint `(Y,U)` laws yield the same conditional count laws for the actual labels; full-box injection yields the same conditional intensity measures Q_i. Pushforward of the latent density ratio through a measurable inverse gives the claimed RN log-ratio Q0-a.e., including on a lower-dimensional image. Since Q0 is common and all Q_i are mutually absolutely continuous, finite intersection of the full-measure sets makes stacking valid. Invertibility of both contrast matrices then makes the affine transformation invertible. The relation is between sufficient statistics evaluated on a common intensity variable and holds Q0-a.e., not pointwise, not for a noisy individual cell, and not necessarily as a componentwise or permutation recovery. A stronger source-identification theorem needs extra assumptions. If alternative label probabilities differ then the observed U marginal differs; equality of the joint law fixes it.

The normal-location fixture is valid: `Z|U=u ∼ N(u,1)`, `q=φ`, T(z)=z, λ(u)=u, ψ(u)=u²/2, and f(z)=(exp z,exp 2z). The two label probabilities are positive, contrast is [1], and the support is the one-dimensional curve `r2=r1²`; ambient intensity density is unnecessary. This is a model, not benchmark data. Distinct assay labels do not themselves guarantee a common decoder or modulation rank; no S1 day labels may be used in learner fitting.

The [pi-VAE supplement §A](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/file/510f2318f324cf07fce24c3a4b89c771-Supplemental.pdf) states that binarizing a Poisson observation yields Bernoulli data and invokes a Bernoulli identification result. Binarization is a valid stochastic transformation, but general Poisson-mixture identification cannot follow solely from its binary marginal: for scalar R, `P(Y>0)=1−E[e^(−R)]` is one number. For example, R=1 and a suitable positive mixture of R=1/2 and R=2 have the same E[e^(−R)] but distinct mixing laws, and therefore distinct full count laws by the injection argument. This example alone is not a counterexample to the entire parametrically restricted pi-VAE theorem; it shows that the generic transformation is lossy and the invoked Bernoulli premise must be independently checked for its exact dimension, labels and restricted family. The RN proof above avoids needing that disputed reduction. No blanket claim of pi-VAE theorem falsehood follows.

## Tractable checks beyond old P01

1. A small rational finite-mixture algebra/Lean lemma: two atomic measures with explicitly pairwise-distinct positive weights and distinct vectors have a unique weight-based cross-assay matching, plus a repeated-weight permutation counterexample. State equality of finite atomic measures as a hypothesis; do not pretend this proves analytic Poisson injection.
2. A matrix lemma `rank(D)=q`, `[A B]` invertible implies `ker(Mᵀ)=ker(Bᵀ)` for `M=BD`, and W spanning that kernel gives `WA` invertible; verify the supplied 2×2 fixture exactly over rationals. The probabilistic count composition remains separately stated.
3. A finite-dimensional algebra lemma for D: invertible L and L̃ plus equal affine contrast vectors implies an invertible affine map of statistic vectors. This checks only the linear step, not RN derivatives or analytic inversion. All fixtures are deterministic, not new data or benchmark worlds.

These checks do not replace independent analysis of Laplace uniqueness, measure pushforward and applicability. Population identifiability does not establish finite-sample estimation, and a measure-zero counterexample cannot be substituted for the a.e. equivalence stated in D.
