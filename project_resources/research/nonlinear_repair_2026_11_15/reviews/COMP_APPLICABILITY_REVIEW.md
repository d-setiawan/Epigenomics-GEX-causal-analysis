# CoMP: differing assay coordinates and count observation

Independent applicability check, 2026-11-15. The mathematical condition in [Foster et al. (2022), Theorem 2 and Appendix E.1, PDF pp. 18–20](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/foster22a/foster22a.pdf) **does not require coordinate j in the two conditions to have the same biological interpretation**. The paper writes x=A_c z on a common Euclidean vector space and its application examples use common measured features, but the proof uses distinct invertible matrices A_0 and A_1 and the map A_1 A_0^(-1). The same algebra applies to isomorphic finite-dimensional coordinate spaces V_0 and V_1 with fixed, separately labeled bases: A_c maps the same latent vector to assay c, and A_1 A_0^(-1):V_0→V_1. It does not compare like-indexed biological features or need a predetermined genomic pairing. Thus unequal feature semantics alone is not a valid mathematical incompatibility objection; equally sized arrays alone do not establish any of the theorem's other assumptions.

## Restricted count composition

Let S have independent non-Gaussian unit-variance coordinates, with no nonidentity signed permutation preserving its joint distribution. Let D_0,D_1 be nonsingular d×d matrices and let X_c=D_c S with the **same full distribution of S in the two selected assay populations**. Define R_c=exp(X_c) componentwise and let Y_c|R_c have independent Poisson coordinates with mean vector R_c. From each unpaired full count law, the independently reviewed product-Poisson injection recovers the joint distribution of R_c, then log recovers the law of X_c. Under the ICA uniqueness statement invoked by the paper, each D_c is identified up to column signed permutation; requiring the same S law and excluding signed-permutation symmetries forces a common ambiguity. Thus D_1D_0^(-1) is population identified, yielding the intensity-space map

R_1 = exp[D_1D_0^(-1) log R_0].

This is a mathematically possible post-link nonlinear intensity model on differently labeled d-feature panels. It is not a claim that the observed counts satisfy the model, that a finite-sample estimator is accurate, or that a noisy Y_0 is its latent R_0. The full recovered intensity law plus known Poisson kernel provides the posterior law of R_0 conditional on Y_0; pushing it through the identified map gives the corresponding model-based target-intensity posterior. A predictive distribution for a second count vector further requires a specified measurement kernel and appropriate conditional independence of the two observation noises given the common latent state. No actual cell pairing is learned or guaranteed.

The transformation is a same-state structural matching under assumptions. Calling it a causal intervention/counterfactual requires a defensible intervention and invariant structural mechanism, neither supplied just by treating assay identity as a condition. It is not an identified named genomic causal graph.

## Nonvacuity and limits

For d=1 let S=E−1, E~Exponential(rate 1), so S has variance 1, is non-Gaussian, and S and −S have different support/distribution. Let D_0=[1], D_1=[2]. Then R_0=exp(S), R_1=exp(2S)=R_0², and the count laws are proper (although the intensity means may be infinite; Poisson mixing-law injection needs no rate-moment condition). This is an algebraic population fixture, not a new experiment. For finite intensity moments, take a bounded skew unit-variance distribution instead.

The specific S1 local-QC populations need not share their latent law, and historical assays have no verified donor matching. Cross-assay independent source distributions, non-Gaussianity/asymmetry, no other linear symmetry, strict square full rank, equal latent and panel dimension, and the exact log-linear rate model are substantive unverified assumptions. The published no-symmetry exclusion matters: symmetric independent sources permit unobservable sign changes and distinct alignments. Unit-variance is a normalization; source labels remain a joint permutation/sign ambiguity, while the transported intensity map can still be unique. Unknown capture, endogenous depth, alternative observation kernels and feature selection can invalidate the Poisson bridge or population comparison. Full population count-law identification is not the same as a stable finite-count inversion.

The published paper calls the noiseless linear model a simplifying theoretical setting and states the linear decoder as a limitation; its practical neural CVAE experiments do not establish this square log-rate model. [The authors' public repository](https://github.com/BenevolentAI/CoMP) documents a PyTorch conditional-VAE implementation and a shared tabular `features.tsv` interface; inspection here reached its README, not the internal decoder source. A faithful count-native adaptation would need a pinned source audit, an explicit product-Poisson likelihood, condition-specific maps, a model/prior or estimator able to represent asymmetric non-Gaussian independent sources, fixed population/selection and training-only alignment, and validation on the already authorized count inputs. A generic CoMP fit or arbitrary `log1p` preprocessing does not invert the count kernel and does not by itself inherit the theorem. No implementation or fit was run in this check.

This result is a conditional population route under a different model from the original unrestricted content/style theorem, not a claim of universal cross-assay recoverability. Its comparison with previously tested count-source estimators and its feasibility at d=30 require a separately approved plan; the existing C01 data-selection and evaluator restrictions remain in force.
