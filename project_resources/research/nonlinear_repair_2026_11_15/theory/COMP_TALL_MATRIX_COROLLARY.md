# E2: unequal-width, undercomplete count-intensity transport

Astra derivation, 2026-11-15, refining the reviewed [square class E](COMP_COUNT_SHARED_TRANSPORT.md). The [independent review](../reviews/COMP_TALL_INDEPENDENT_REVIEW.md) accepted this corollary within its assumptions. This is a population corollary, with no change to frozen experiments and no claim that the current data satisfy it.

Let the common source S in R^d have mutually independent non-Gaussian unit-variance coordinates and no nonidentity signed-permutation symmetry, as in E. Assay c may have p_c features, with p_c>=d, and M_c in R^(p_c by d) has full column rank. Define log R_c=M_c S and positive R_c coordinatewise by exponentiation; the count observation is the same conditional product-Poisson kernel. All competing representations obey these restrictions, with one common selected-population source law across assays. Neither equal feature widths nor semantic matching of feature coordinates is required.

The observed count law identifies the law of log R_c by the reviewed Poisson bridge and known logarithm. Since Cov(S)=I_d, its covariance C_c=M_c M_c^T exists, has rank d, and has range V_c=col(M_c). Hence the common latent dimension and each log-rate subspace are identified from the population laws. Choose any matrix Q_c whose columns form an orthonormal basis of V_c. The reduced vector Z_c=Q_c^T log R_c has square invertible mixing A_c=Q_c^T M_c. Applying E to these reduced laws identifies the unique invertible linear map T_c: R^d -> R^d carrying law(Z_0) to law(Z_c).

Lift it to U_c: V_0 -> V_c by U_c(v)=Q_c T_c Q_0^T v. This map is independent of the chosen bases: changing bases conjugates the reduced transports and cancels on lifting. Equivalently it is the unique linear isomorphism of the identified subspaces carrying one log-rate law to the other, because any alternative would produce a forbidden linear symmetry of S. Thus F_c(r)=exp(U_c(log r)) is the identified transport on exp(V_0), in particular almost surely on the source rate law. The shared-state intensity-joint and additional count-noise conditional-independence conclusions of E carry over unchanged.

There is generally no unique matrix extension from all of R^(p_0) to R^(p_c): any matrix vanishing on V_0 can be added without changing observations or the identified restricted map. Q_c T_c Q_0^T is a convenient canonical extension that vanishes on V_0's orthogonal complement, not an identified biological extrapolation off the supported subspace. The target is a support/subspace transport, not an arbitrary ambient matrix or a noisy cell's realized state.

**Nonvacuity.** In dimension d=1 choose S~Exponential(rate1), M_0=(-1,-2)^T and M_1=(-3,-4,-5)^T. Both matrices have full column rank. The two-feature and three-feature rate vectors have positive bounded coordinates and nonlinear relations, with common S; log-rate covariance rank is1 in both assays. The restricted map sends t(-1,-2) to t(-3,-4,-5). This is an analytic fixture, not newly generated benchmark observations.

**Important boundary.** We use covariance only to recover the subspace; we do not discard means or silently add assay-specific intercepts. The theorem is a linear log-rate model with a possibly nonzero source mean. Extending to arbitrary affine log-rate maps requires revisiting affine source symmetries: a distribution without a linear sign symmetry can still have a reflection about its nonzero mean. Finite covariance of log rates follows from the source normalization; finite moments of the rates themselves are not required for the observation-channel injection. Stable finite-count estimation of the log-rate law/covariance is not supplied by this population argument. Current count fits C01–C03 do not implement or validate E2; a count-native estimator and its structural/source-law assumptions remain a separate algorithmic dependency.
