# E: a nonvacuous shared nonlinear count-intensity transport class

Astra statement, 2026-11-15; based on the independent [CoMP applicability review](../reviews/COMP_APPLICABILITY_REVIEW.md). The [independent correspondence review](../reviews/COMP_COUNT_TRANSPORT_INDEPENDENT_REVIEW.md) accepted this statement within its assumptions. This restricts the shared-state/measurement model substantively rather than retaining the impossible all-measurable domain-variability premise. It is not the unrestricted original content/style theorem, and has no private latent block.

**Observation and model.** Assays c=0,...,C have vectors of the same finite dimension d, whose coordinates need not have corresponding biological meanings. For the selected population let S have mutually independent, non-Gaussian, unit-variance coordinates and no nonidentity signed-permutation distributional symmetry. Let M_c be invertible d by d matrices and define L_c=M_c S, R_c=exp(L_c) coordinatewise. Conditional on R_c, Y_c has independent Poisson coordinates with rates R_c. The learner observes only the separate count-vector laws P(Y_c), not their cross-assay joint or any realized latent rate. Every competing representation must satisfy the same model restrictions, including one common selected-population source law across its assays and the same dimension. The common law need not be known. This is the square linear-ICA class after a known inverse link, with a nonlinear raw-rate map and noisy count observations.

**Identified target.** The laws P(R_c) and P(L_c) and the unique linear transport T_c=M_c M_0^{-1} taking P(L_0) to P(L_c) are identified. Consequently the deterministic intensity transport is F_c(r)=exp(T_c log(r)). A particular noisy cell's R_c is not recovered exactly. Source coordinates remain subject to the common ICA representation equivalence; a transport between observed assay coordinate systems is a different target.

**Proof.** Full product-Poisson injection first identifies each rate law; the known coordinatewise logarithm then identifies each log-rate law. In this square ICA class, the no-signed-permutation-symmetry premise, together with independence, non-Gaussianity and unit variances, rules out a nonidentity invertible linear distributional symmetry of S. This is exactly the linear symmetry argument underlying [CoMP Theorem 2 and Appendix E.1](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/foster22a/foster22a.pdf). Equivalently one may invoke that conditional theorem directly on the identified log-rate laws.

For clarity, once the symmetry property is established, transport uniqueness is elementary. If another invertible T sends P(L_0) to P(L_c), then H=M_c^{-1} T M_0 preserves P(S). Hence H is identity and T=M_c M_0^{-1}. This characterizes the transport entirely by observed-law-derived log-rate marginals and the stipulated model class. Exponentiation gives F_c. Repeating relative to assay0 gives consistent transports for all assays. No cell pairing, common feature semantics, observed auxiliary modulation or count binarization is used in this proof.

**Joint completion requires its own model statement.** Under the common S structural model, all intensities satisfy R_c=F_c(R_0). Their joint intensity law is therefore the pushforward of identified P(R_0). If count observation noise is additionally mutually independent across assays conditional on the common S, the same-cell count joint is the mixture of the product Poisson kernels over that joint rate law and is identified. Within-assay Poisson independence alone does not supply this cross-assay noise assumption. Without it, residual count couplings remain unidentified. Neither the transport nor this conditional joint is automatically a biological intervention or a directed genomic causal graph.

**Nonvacuity.** In dimension one choose S~Exponential(rate1), with variance1 and no sign symmetry, M_0=[-1], M_1=[-2]. Then R_0=exp(-S) is uniform on(0,1), R_1=R_0^2, and the map is nonlinear in rate space. Both are strictly positive, both Poisson observations have finite moments of every order, and the intensity transport is uniquely identified in the stated class. This is an analytic fixture, not a generated benchmark. Higher-dimensional examples use independent gamma coordinates of different shapes, scaled to variance1; their positive supports exclude sign flips and their distinct marginal distributions exclude nontrivial permutations. Suitable invertible negative diagonal maps yield bounded positive rates.

**Current-input applicability and estimator boundary.** The existing first30 and full300 assay panels have equal widths, so different feature names alone do not obstruct this theorem. Common selected-population law is not established after assay-specific QC; square full-rank log-linear mixing, independent asymmetric sources and absence of private factors are strong unverified assumptions. The proof gives population identification, not a stable way to estimate log-rate moments from finite counts or certify a global optimum. The published practical CoMP model is a conditional variational estimator; native count likelihood and exact architecture correspondence must be audited separately before any faithful application or proposed adaptation. A Gaussian reconstruction objective on log1p observed counts is not the Poisson–log-rate model proved here. C01's additive-intensity finite mixtures and C02's sparse binary model do not empirically validate E.

The fixed-known-shape NB bridge could replace the Poisson channel after its independent review, with known shapes in every competing model. Unknown dispersion and endogenous count-derived depth are not covered. No claim that the original broad theorem was repaired merely by changing null-set notation is made.
