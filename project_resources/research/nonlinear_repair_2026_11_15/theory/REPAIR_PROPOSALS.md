# Nonvacuous alternatives for unpaired content identification

Author's proposal, 2026-11-15. This is a population-level mathematical proposal for independent review and Astra's study-plan decision, not a claim that the empirical assumptions hold or that finite-sample computation has been performed. The two constructions below are different model classes. The first repairs a restricted continuous content-identification statement, not the full Shrestha–Fu unknown-dimension content/style theorem. The second is a finite-state count alternative, not a special case of smooth continuous content–style identification. Neither proves a causal graph.

## Observation and target boundary

The learner observes one joint count distribution per assay (or independent observations thereof), feature identities and fixed permitted annotations. Samples across assays are unpaired. No shared state labels, cell links, evaluator-only focal outcomes, depth truth, causal truth, or test-reference values may influence fitting or model selection. A shared selected population is an assumption, not a consequence of random assay assignment: S1 assay-local QC differs and its all-assay-QC reference has another selection event. The detailed [observation contract](../../research_program_2026_11_14/theory/OBSERVATION_TARGET_CONTRACT.md) governs each proposed application. Historical datasets lack verified donor matching.

The target in the continuous proposition is the rank of a scalar common content variable in a noiseless invertible observation model; in the count proposition it is the weight-aligned mixture component laws and per-assay posterior membership probabilities, not realized cell labels or a same-cell joint. Neither identifies named genomic causal effects.

## Why the unrestricted variability premise needs replacement

The [prior review](../../nonlinear_recheck_2026_11_14/SKEPTICAL_ASSUMPTION_REVIEW.md) proves both the common-null-point contradiction and a stronger obstruction. For N finite domains with atomless style laws μ_n, Lyapunov's finite-dimensional vector-measure convexity theorem gives a measurable H with μ_n(H)=1/2 for every n: the vector measure's range contains its endpoints 0 and 1 and their midpoint. If content and style are independent within each domain, A=C×H has probability 1/2 everywhere and differs from every measurable B×S by probability 1/2 everywhere. Hence an a.e. change alone still cannot make the original *all measurable sets* premise hold for this class. See [Khan and Sagara, Theorem 2](https://www.econ2.jhu.edu/REPEC/papers/wp586_khan.pdf) and [Shrestha–Fu, Assumption 3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.03755). The propositions below replace the class or restriction and must be assessed independently.

## Option A: a restricted continuous encoder with observable moment contrast

**Model and population information.** Two unpaired domain laws of X_n=g(C,S_n) share the same invertible g on the union of product supports; C has the same continuous strictly increasing CDF F_C in both, and E|S_n|<∞. No sample pairing is used. Assume E S_1≠E S_2. Assume the scalar candidate encoder belongs to the restricted family e(g(c,s))=h(c)+βs, where h is strictly increasing and integrable and β is a scalar shared across domains. This is a genuine structural restriction on admissible encoders relative to the unobserved true coordinates, not a generally testable property from unmatched X. The candidate is required to have the same distribution in both domains. The target is U=F_C(C), equivalent to C up to its increasing reparameterization.

**Proposition.** Every such matched encoder has β=0 and F_{e(X)}(e(X))=F_C(C) almost surely in both domains. Proof: distribution equality implies equality of expectations; equality of the content marginal cancels E h(C), yielding β(E S_1−E S_2)=0, hence β=0. Since h is strictly increasing, F_{h(C)}(h(c))=F_C(c) on the support. Thus the recovered rank is domain-invariant and excludes a style-dependent encoding within the specified family. No all-measurable-set diversity assumption is involved.

**Nonvacuity fixture.** C and S_1 independent Uniform(0,1), S_2 independent Uniform(1,2), g(c,s)=(c+s,s), h(c)=c², and e(x)=h(x_1−x_2). The contrast in means equals 1; e matches distributions and yields U=C. The same construction works for dependent C,S_n provided the stated common marginal and moments hold. This fixture is deterministic algebra, not a generated benchmark or proof of biological fit.

**Limitations and next obligations.** The permitted encoder family is substantially narrower than arbitrary nonlinear encoders; the scalar β assumption is not assured for a neural network, and unknown g makes the restriction hard to verify or enforce without additional structure. Vector styles need a full-rank matrix of mean contrasts and sufficient domains; an arbitrary nonlinear style term needs a separately justified finite-dimensional class and full-rank contrast matrix. This theorem recovers neither style nor dimension and assumes noiseless invertible X; noisy Poisson counts are not covered. A known injective observation kernel may identify the distribution of X, but does not restore the realized latent X or a per-cell encoder from a noisy count. Consequently, no application to current counts is claimed.

## Option B: distinct-weight finite-state Poisson count theorem

**Model.** For assay a=1,…,A, the permitted population law is that of unpaired count vector Y_a∈N_0^{p_a}. A common finite state Z∈{1,…,K} has positive pairwise-distinct probabilities w_k shared across assay populations. Conditional on Z=k, counts are independent across features with Y_{aj}~Poisson(λ_{akj}), with λ_{akj}≥0. For each assay, one prespecified feature j_a has K pairwise-distinct rates. States can affect each assay's feature rates arbitrarily: there is no assumed linear map, feature correspondence or shared response ordering between assays. Equivalently, λ_{ak}=f_a(k) for arbitrary nonnegative vector-valued responses. The state probabilities and emissions are unknown. Fixed known positive exposure can be absorbed into the rates, or divided out if its value is known. This is an identifiable finite-state alternative rather than a repaired continuous theorem.

**Proposition.** The marginal count law in each assay determines K and the unordered tuples (w_k,λ_{ak}); pairwise-distinct common weights align those tuples uniquely across assays, up to one joint permutation. Consequently each assay's model posterior P(Z=k|Y_a=y) and each aligned response vector are identified. The realized state of a particular cell is generally not determined because Poisson component supports overlap. Without an additional assumption about coupling conditional on Z, the same-cell joint distribution remains unidentified.

**Proof.** Take any one assay and write its probability-generating function G(z)=Σ_k w_k exp[Σ_j λ_kj(z_j−1)]. Set all non-anchor coordinates to 1. The resulting finite sum Σ_k w_k exp[λ_k1(z_1−1)] uniquely determines the finite set of distinct λ_k1 and associated positive weights: a finite linear combination of exponentials with distinct real exponents that vanishes on an open interval is identically zero, and sequential division by the largest exponential as its argument tends to infinity forces every coefficient to zero. Analytic continuation permits that argument from the observed PGF interval; the finite exponential sum is entire. For each other feature j, differentiate G with respect to z_j and set z_{−1}=1. The result is Σ_k w_k λ_kj exp[λ_k1(z_1−1)]. Uniqueness of coefficients for the already identified exponents yields each λ_kj. Repeat for every assay and align the component with the same unique weight. Bayes' rule gives P(Z=k|y) = w_k ∏_j Pois(y_j;λ_kj)/Σ_l w_l ∏_j Pois(y_j;λ_lj) wherever the denominator is positive. This establishes exact population identification within the stated model, not estimator consistency or stable estimation for finite n.

**Deterministic algebra fixture.** K=3, weights (0.2,0.3,0.5); RNA rates ((1,5),(3,2),(6,4)); chromatin rates ((4,1),(1,7),(8,3)). First-feature rates differ within each assay, and response orders differ between assays. Weight 0.2 matches RNA (1,5) to chromatin (4,1), 0.3 matches (3,2) to (1,7), and 0.5 matches (6,4) to (8,3). This is a concrete model specification and algebraic illustration, not a new simulated dataset, fitted result or a validated biological class.

**Kernel composition.** If independent capture thins each Poisson feature with fixed known q_aj>0, observed counts remain Poisson(q_aj λ_akj) and rate recovery divides by q_aj; unknown feature-wise q gives only effective rates, though distinct weights may still align if the anchor rates remain distinct. Known constant exposure acts similarly. More generally, to compose with another count, NB, zero-inflation or depth kernel, explicitly prove that its map from the relevant mixture measure to the observed law is injective over the proposed class and that component weights remain identifiable; a known kernel alone does not guarantee injectivity. Zero capture destroys that feature's information. Random or state-dependent exposures, estimated endogenous library sizes, unknown dropout and selection can change or conflate mixture weights and invalidate the argument. A retained-count row sum is not automatically exogenous exposure. Exact PGF equality and analytic continuation are population statements and can be ill-conditioned in sparse finite samples.

**Executable fit candidate and applicability gate.** A product-Poisson finite-mixture likelihood is directly evaluable using the existing permitted frozen count matrices; no pairing or evaluator truth is required. It is a new estimator proposal, not the existing factorial-cumulant linear ICA estimator, and it must not be described as validating the current count-source algorithm or assuming the actual generator is a finite Poisson mixture. Before any fitting, Astra should freeze population/selection, panels, anchor rule using training data only, candidate K and optimization/initialization, label alignment, model-misspecification diagnostics, weight-separation uncertainty and heldout count likelihood, and a separate evaluation plan. Close or repeated weights, nearly identical emissions, overdispersion and local optima may make a mathematically identified model empirically unreliable. Holdout paired cells and labels remain evaluation-only and cannot select K, restarts or thresholds. Whether common post-QC state weights are defensible for S1 is unresolved; a poor fit or common-weight violation motivates abstention rather than forced alignment. Evaluation with independently fitted reference populations needs an explicit transport argument.

## Competition and decision

Option A is a genuine nonvacuous continuous identification claim but has an oracle-like encoder restriction that lacks an established count implementation. Option B supplies a fully specified likelihood for existing nonnegative count matrices and a proof of what unpaired population laws identify, at the cost of a finite-state/product-Poisson model, distinct shared weights, and a common population. Option B is preferable for a proposed training-only count-model check if the selection and population assumptions can be explicitly motivated; it does not inherit prior experiments' validity or answer causal-graph recovery. A separate finite-count proof review and an observation-model/selection review are required before treating either proposal as established project theory. No data or experiments were run for this document.

## Follow-up C: observable mean-contrast continuous repair with count composition

This strengthens Option A's oracle-defined encoder family. The construction is available when **domains measure the same feature coordinates**, with the same known invertible feature link; it does not assume heterogeneous assays share coordinates. Let n=1,…,N index such domains, and let V_n=A C_n+B S_n∈R^d, with A∈R^{d×p}, B∈R^{d×q}, d=p+q, [A B] full rank, and all first moments finite. Each C_n has the same distribution (not necessarily independent of S_n). Let the q×(N−1) matrix D=[E S_2−E S_1,…,E S_N−E S_1] have rank q, requiring N≥q+1. Observations are either the law of V_n directly, or the joint count law specified below. The known feature link σ:R→(0,∞) is applied componentwise; exp is a concrete example. Parameters A,B and latent coordinates are unknown.

**Identification claim and proof.** The observable mean-contrast matrix M=[E V_2−E V_1,…,E V_N−E V_1] equals B D; since B has full column rank and D has full row rank, col(M)=col(B). Choose any full-row-rank W∈R^{p×d} whose rows span the observable left nullspace of M. Then WB=0 and WA is invertible: if WA v=0, Av belongs to col(B), so [A B] full rank forces v=0. Hence U_n=W V_n=(WA)C_n, and U's law is identified identically across domains up to invertible linear coordinates. Any other basis W' of the same left nullspace equals T W for invertible T. Likewise, every observationally equivalent representation satisfying these assumptions yields content related to the original by an invertible linear transformation. The data-derived admissible linear encoder class {wᵀV:wᵀM=0} is enforceable in population and excludes style contributions for this model. This identifies an abstract quotient representation, not A, B themselves, named biological factors, style, causality, or common same-cell pairings.

**Nonvacuity algebra fixture.** p=q=1, d=2, A=(1,1)ᵀ, B=(1,−1)ᵀ. Let C~Uniform(−1,1) in each domain, S_1~Uniform(−1,1), S_2~Uniform(0,2), with any coupling preserving these marginals, and V_n=(C+S_n,C−S_n). Then M=(1,−1)ᵀ, W=(1/2,1/2), W V_n=C. With Λ_n=exp(V_n) and conditionally independent Y_{nj}~Poisson(Λ_{nj}), this defines a concrete nonlinear-link count model with finite positive rates; it is an algebraic specification, not a new simulated world or an experiment.

**Poisson kernel composition.** For arbitrary probability law P_Λ on [0,∞)^d and conditionally independent Poisson counts given Λ, the count PGF G(z)=E[∏z_j^{Y_j}]=L_Λ(1−z), for all real z∈(0,1)^d. If two count laws agree then their Laplace transforms agree on the full real open box (0,1)^d. Each transform is separately holomorphic for complex arguments with strictly positive real parts. Holding d−1 coordinates in the real open box and applying the one-variable identity theorem successively in each coordinate extends equality to the full product of right half-planes. For arbitrary t∈R^d, approach ε1−it from inside that product and apply dominated convergence as ε↓0; the characteristic functions agree, so the Λ laws agree. Thus the Poisson kernel identifies the entire mixing distribution, not individual Λ values. Under Λ_j>0 a.s., the known bijection log identifies the law of V; under the stated integrability, its means and M are therefore identified. The induced joint law P_Λ(dλ)∏_j Pois(y_j;λ_j) identifies the conditional law of U=W log Λ given observed counts, for y with positive probability, modulo the same GL(p) basis choice. It does not imply deterministic per-cell content recovery, pointwise measurement inversion, numerical stability, or feasibility at finite sample size.

The same proof extends to a known componentwise bijection σ with measurable inverse, and known fixed positive capture/exposure by division before σ⁻¹. Unknown endogenous library size or selection-dependent count truncation is not covered. In this project there is no demonstrated common coordinate system, common generator, or suitable sequence of same-measurement domains: RNA and histone feature spaces differ, and the local-QC cohorts can have different selected content laws. Do not manufacture domains from these assays or claim them as instances of the proposition. A potential within-assay same-feature contrast requires verified permitted group labels, adequate independent groups and a prespecified population argument.

## Stronger finite mixture: anchor feature unnecessary

The Poisson injection result above removes Option B's single anchor requirement. If each assay has a finite mixing law P_{Λ_a}=Σ_{k=1}^K w_k δ_{λ_{ak}}, with all λ_{ak} vectors pairwise distinct within that assay, the full count law identifies the atomic probability measure and therefore its K distinct rate vectors and positive masses. Pairwise-distinct weights shared across the assay populations align atoms uniquely across assays, up to a common permutation. The count likelihood and posterior formula are unchanged. Distinct vectors need not differ in any single common feature: e.g. (1,1), (1,2), (2,1) are three distinct two-feature rate vectors but no coordinate separates all three. Equal rate vectors merge into one atom; repeated weights obstruct unique weight alignment; neither issue is cured by infinite observations. This is a strictly less restrictive population theorem, although a practical estimator may require stronger separation. The previous single-anchor proof remains a valid narrower elementary proof and fixture.

## López et al. 2024: primary theorem and count-proof check

In [López et al., *Toward the Identifiability of Comparative Deep Generative Models*](https://web.stanford.edu/group/pritchardlab/publications/pdfs/Lopez24.pdf), Theorem 1 (printed p. 872) uses a common continuous injective piecewise-affine mixing function, isotropic Gaussian latent structure and target/background data, to claim subspace disentanglement. Appendix C.3, Theorem 3 (printed p. 892) states its multi-group analogue with common observed features and distinct private blocks. These are not a theorem for arbitrary heterogeneous assay maps, and their structural proof depends on the cited piecewise-affine DGM identifiability result; it has not been independently audited here. Theorem 2 (printed p. 873) claims a reduction from Poisson or specified negative-binomial count observations to noiseless latent-intensity laws under a bicontinuous link; the natural conclusion is equality in distribution, not equality of the same random variables pointwise.

Appendix D.2 Proposition 2 (printed pp. 895–896) relies on equality on a diagonal sequence and then invokes multivariable analytic continuation. Agreement on a sequence, even one with an interior accumulation point, is insufficient in several complex variables (e.g. f(z_1,z_2)=z_1−z_2 and g=0 agree on the entire diagonal but differ). The displayed PDF extraction also shows sign/domain inconsistencies, including a claimed positive real point inside a left half-plane; this is recorded as a proof-presentation concern, not a refutation of the proposition. The full-box coordinatewise proof above independently establishes the **Poisson** mixing-distribution conclusion for the stated conditional product-Poisson kernel, without invoking that diagonal step. It does not, by itself, establish the paper's negative-binomial case under every shape/mean parameterization; its parameterization and proof require a separate full check. The invertible-link step is an ordinary measurable pushforward of equal laws. López et al. give a substantially broader published piecewise-affine nonlinear model class than Option C's post-link affine structure, but their common feature space and group design are unverified for the current cross-assay task. Option B instead admits distinct assay feature spaces and arbitrary finite-state response vectors, paid for by finite states, identified within-assay emissions and distinct common weights.

**Selection advice:** Prefer Option B for an implementable candidate on the existing unequal assay panels, pending a frozen training-only plan and a defensible population selection argument. Use Option C as the cleaner continuous recovery theorem and Poisson composition result, not as an unsupported description of the existing different-feature assay cohorts. López's count reduction supports the same general population-information direction, but its nonlinear subspace theorem needs its own full assumption audit before an empirical reproduction claim. None of these results validates existing factorial-ICA fits, guarantees finite-sample recovery or identifies the cross-assay causal graph.

## Option D: nonlinear Poisson auxiliary-variable theorem via measure ratios

This repairs the Poisson reduction route for a **different** published auxiliary-variable model; it does not repair the original unrestricted content/style variability assumption. It uses a direct likelihood-ratio proof on the latent intensity measure, avoiding both a Bernoulli reduction and any density of an undercomplete intensity manifold with respect to ambient Lebesgue measure. Related primary references: [Zhou and Wei, pi-VAE Theorem 1](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/file/510f2318f324cf07fce24c3a4b89c771-Paper.pdf), its [supplement §A](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/file/510f2318f324cf07fce24c3a4b89c771-Supplemental.pdf), and [Khemakhem et al., iVAE Theorem 1](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v108/khemakhem20a/khemakhem20a.pdf). The proof below states its own sufficient assumptions and conclusion; it does not depend on the withdrawn discrete-observation argument.

**Assumptions and observation.** Let U be an observed auxiliary label taking K=mk+1 prespecified values u_0,…,u_{mk}, each with positive probability. For each value, let Z∈R^m have density p(z|u)=q(z) exp{λ(u)ᵀT(z)−ψ(u)} relative to Lebesgue measure, where q(z)>0 for Lebesgue-a.e. z on a common support Ω, T(z) is an R^{mk}-valued statistic (in the iVAE/pi-VAE class its m blocks are coordinate-wise sufficient statistics), and ψ(u) is finite. Let R=f(Z)∈(0,∞)^d with a measurable injective f whose inverse on its measurable image is measurable; d≥m is allowed, including d>m. Conditional on R, the d count coordinates are independent Poisson with respective rates R_j, with the kernel common across U. The learner observes the joint law of (Y,U), not Z, R or pairings. Let L=[λ(u_1)−λ(u_0),…,λ(u_{mk})−λ(u_0)] be an invertible mk×mk matrix. Assume a competing parameterization has the same form, dimension mk, common-support positivity, measurable injective decoder and invertible contrast matrix, and produces the same joint (Y,U) law.

**Claim.** The sufficient-statistic representations on the common intensity law agree up to one invertible affine transformation: T(f⁻¹(r))=M \tilde T(\tilde f⁻¹(r))+b for P_{R|u0}-almost every r, where M is invertible and b is fixed across labels. This is the general *linear equivalence of sufficient statistics*, not automatically componentwise latent-source identification, an equality of decoders, or deterministic recovery of a latent cell from one noisy count. Stronger componentwise/permutation results require additional hypotheses from the relevant iVAE theorem and separate checking.

**Proof.** Positive-probability labels and equality of joint (Y,U) laws imply equality of each conditional Y law. The full-box Poisson argument above implies equal conditional intensity probability measures Q_i=P_{R|u_i}=\tilde P_{R|u_i}, including when supported on a lower-dimensional manifold. Positivity on a common Ω makes the latent laws mutually absolutely continuous; injectivity gives for Q_0-a.e. r

log(dQ_i/dQ_0)(r) = [λ(u_i)−λ(u_0)]ᵀ T(f⁻¹(r)) − [ψ(u_i)−ψ(u_0)].

The same identity holds for the competing model, with the same Radon–Nikodym derivative because the Q_i are equal. Stack i=1,…,mk and write Lᵀ T_f(r)−a=\tilde Lᵀ \tilde T_{\tilde f}(r)−\tilde a. Invertibility of both L and \tilde L yields T_f(r)=(Lᵀ)⁻¹\tilde Lᵀ \tilde T_{\tilde f}(r)+(Lᵀ)⁻¹(a−\tilde a). The matrix is invertible. Finite many labels permit intersection of all full-measure sets. No ambient intensity density, Jacobian determinant, or extension from an uncountable null-set union is needed. A standard-Borel injective f guarantees the required measurable inverse on its image; we state it explicitly. For continuously distributed U, conditional laws are only defined almost everywhere, so the selected labels require additional continuity/version assumptions; the positive-probability-label formulation avoids that issue.

**Nonvacuity fixture.** m=k=1, U∈{0,1} with both probabilities positive, Z|U=u~Normal(u,1). Then q(z)=(2π)^(−1/2)exp(−z²/2), T(z)=z, λ(u)=u and ψ(u)=u²/2; L=[1]. Let f(z)=(exp z,exp(2z)) and Y_j|Z independently Poisson(f_j(Z)). The nonlinear injective rate map is undercomplete, with support on the curve r_2=r_1²; nevertheless the intensity measure ratio is well-defined, and the theorem yields T_f(r)=log r_1 up to affine equivalence across alternative admissible models. This is an analytic model fixture, not a new generated world.

**Relevance gate.** A real observed auxiliary variable whose conditional latent natural-parameter contrasts have rank mk is required. For an auxiliary with only K labels, mk≤K−1 is necessary. An assay label alone is not a substitute for a common count-coordinate measurement map, and if supposedly shared content is invariant to that label then its natural-parameter rows do not vary and the full-rank requirement fails. A retained-count depth proxy is not a verified independent or known exposure. Conditional distributions and auxiliary labels must be part of permitted learner observations before using this model. Within an appropriate single assay, a genuinely observed biological or technical auxiliary might be assessable if authorized and selected population semantics are clear, but S1 day labels and paired cell links are evaluator-only under the existing contract. The theorem gives sufficient-statistic equivalence in each single common measurement model; it does not identify alignment of different assay decoders, a same-cell joint, or a causal graph.

**Tradeoff for Astra.** D supplies a genuinely nonlinear continuous count-population theorem with a constructive proof and avoids a finite-state approximation, but requires an observed informative auxiliary and restrictive conditional exponential-family modulation. B requires no auxiliary and accommodates different feature spaces, but assumes finite shared states, distinct shared weights and within-assay identifiable emissions. C uses domain mean contrasts and an observable annihilator in one common feature space; it permits dependence and yields an explicitly constructed content quotient, but is post-link linear. None is a justified fit to the present S1 heterogeneous assays without a model-specific selection and measurement audit. No estimator was fitted.
