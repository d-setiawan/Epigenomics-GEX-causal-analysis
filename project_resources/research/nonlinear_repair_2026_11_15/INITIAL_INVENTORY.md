# Starting inventory and open candidate leads

Status 2026-11-15: discovery, not completed review. Prior bounded recheck includes Kong, Xie, Yan, Nguyen–Fu, Xu, Yao, Zheng–Zhang, Sturma, Timilsina, Shrestha; revisit sources and proofs in light of the count-assay contract. The literature review from 2026-11-14 contains detailed prior observations and must not be treated as sealed validation of new claims.

1. Finite latent mixture: within each assay use conditionally independent feature blocks and Kruskal full-rank conditions for component identification; distinct common class weights can align independently identified classes as a separate derivation. Conditional cross-assay independence is additionally required to infer a joint from class-specific marginals. Same selected class proportions and finite components are unverified; ties leave label ambiguity. A count distribution can use discrete block outcomes, but high dimensionality alone establishes no ranks or conditional independence. Validation proposal: compare held-out within-assay block moments and cross-assay fitted weight intervals under a prespecified model; these checks cannot prove selection invariance or conditional cross-assay independence. Prior established theorem-specific attribution is Allman et al. Corollary 2, not their continuous Theorem 8.
2. Count operator route: identify the mixing law of Poisson intensity vectors from population count laws, then inspect whether a nonlinear shared-intensity model makes cross-assay alignment identifiable. Measurement invertibility alone does not identify the coupling of two mixing laws; latent nonlinear maps and selection remain unresolved. Need primary uniqueness result, domain-specific variation assumptions, estimator and finite-sample stability.
3. Gulrajani & Hashimoto 2022, https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/gulrajani22a.html, domain map identification through distribution asymmetries and third-moment certificates: discovery-stage lead, exact theorem and count compatibility still under inspection. Equal dimensional linear mapping, not yet shown valid for different assay measurement models.
4. Noisy nonlinear ICA, https://www.imo.universite-paris-saclay.fr/~elisabeth.gassiat/Disentangling.pdf: discovery-stage lead; uses structured observations and additive noise, so review whether existing cells have required structure and whether count observation kernels can replace additive noise.

A positive population class does not establish that the existing data-generating process belongs to it. No fitting is approved by this inventory.

## iVAE discrete-observation version discrepancy

[Khemakhem et al. 2020 PMLR published version](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v108/khemakhem20a/khemakhem20a.pdf), abstract and page 3, claims identifiability extends to discrete observations using an infinitesimal-temperature limit. The authors' [later arXiv v4, Appendix C](https://arxiv.org/html/1907.04809) explicitly says the discrete case requires a bespoke identifiability proof and provides simulation evidence instead. Thus neither an automatic exclusion of all discrete models nor a direct Poisson observation theorem follows from this paper. An injective multivariate Poisson mixing operator could potentially furnish an alternate proof step, but must be proved for the exact model and across domains; this is an open composition, not an existing theorem. Its exponential-family prior, observed auxiliary variables with sufficiently many distinct parameter contrasts and shared generator remain separate assumptions.

## Gulrajani–Hashimoto primary screen

[ICML 2022, Propositions 3.1, 3.3 and 3.5](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/gulrajani22a/gulrajani22a.pdf), printed pp. 3–4: conditional on admissible invertible linear maps and absence of source-distribution linear symmetries, the map is unique. Proposition 3.3 supplies sufficient conditions for absence of orthogonal symmetries in a whitened distribution: a unique CP decomposition of the third moment, unequal unit-normalized weights, and full multilinear rank. Proposition 3.5 supplies a random-transformation certificate under stated sampling/algorithm conditions. This concerns map uniqueness rather than verification that such a map is biological: different feature widths and stochastic assay-specific count kernels do not automatically satisfy an invertible linear mapping. A count-native model might instead target compatible latent intensity coordinates but requires a new composition theorem. Existing-input validation would first test dimension, count likelihood and stability of the putative map and its moment/rank diagnostics; no proposed check confirms the cross-assay biological map or finite-sample CP uniqueness without further assumptions. Official code and detailed proof remain to inspect.

## Count-native nonlinear results newly found (primary theorem inspected; proof audits pending)

**Lopez et al. (CLeaR 2024), [Toward the Identifiability of Comparative Deep Generative Models](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v236/lopez24a/lopez24a.pdf), Theorem 2, Appendix D.2.** With standard Gaussian latent u, f=σ∘g where g is piecewise affine and σ is a coordinatewise bicontinuous bijection to positive rates, identical Poisson or fixed-shape negative-binomial observation laws imply identical f(u) and g(u) laws. This is a published count-noise bridge to continuous-law analysis, not itself cross-assay coupling or individual-cell recovery. Their comparative Theorems 1 and 3 additionally use the paper's structured multi-group intervention model and shared injective piecewise affine decoder on common observation coordinates, not supplied merely by different assay identities. Equations of the model and proof need further primary audit. Candidate existing-input validation: specify a count model and use strictly held-out within-assay posterior predictive marginals/moments; compare nuisance depth assumptions and fitted residual structure. These cannot verify global injectivity, shared biological coordinates or absent cross-assay dependence. No fit approved.

**Zhou & Wei (NeurIPS 2020), [Learning identifiable and interpretable latent models of high-dimensional neural activity using pi-VAE](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2020/file/510f2318f324cf07fce24c3a4b89c771-Paper.pdf), Theorem 1.** Claims identification up to an invertible linear transformation of sufficient-statistic coordinates for Poisson observations with injective rate map, factorial exponential-family prior conditional on observed per-observation u, nonzero sufficient-statistic derivatives a.e., and invertible mk-dimensional matrix of mk auxiliary-parameter contrasts. This is not an individual-component or cross-assay alignment claim. Two assay IDs give at most one independent contrast and distinct assay features need not share a decoder; u may include other permitted observed covariates only if present and valid. Estimator: ELBO optimization with injectable flow and amortized posterior; finite fit does not prove global optimum or latent premises. Inspect distributional residuals, contrast conditioning and held-out predictive fit under a prespecified plan, without treating them as proofs. Supplement proof and code audit pending: the publisher's supplement is 15 MB and web retrieval failed; a search snippet suggests Bernoulli identification may be involved and Lopez et al. exhibit Bernoulli counterexamples, so do not treat the claimed proof as verified. The official code is https://github.com/zhd96/pi-vae; code-only retrieval requested.

**Lopez proof caution.** Appendix D.2 Proposition 2 states the broader count-to-intensity-law step for arbitrary nonnegative random rate vectors (no Gaussian or piecewise-affine restriction), using independent-coordinate Poisson or fixed-shape NB kernels; the intended conclusion is equality in distribution, as its proof states. The displayed statement appears to omit the distribution superscript. Its printed proof cites analytic continuation after noting equality on a diagonal sequence; in several complex variables that sequence alone is not a valid uniqueness set. The proof also gives equality over a coordinatewise product set, which may permit a repaired coordinate-by-coordinate identity-theorem argument, but this must be independently checked. The result is not a pointwise identification of the rate function or joint latent coupling. Appendix C.3 Theorem 3 assumes one common continuous injective piecewise-affine decoder on the overlapping coordinate subspaces (z,t1,0) and (z,0,t2), with isotropic Gaussian latent blocks, and identifies the shared/private subspaces subject to the paper's equivalence. Distinct feature spaces do not supply that premise automatically.

**pi-VAE proof audit update.** Authors' [arXiv v1 Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.04798) reproduces the full proof: it thresholds each Poisson count to a Bernoulli outcome and cites the earlier iVAE Bernoulli identifiability claim. The later iVAE v4 says a discrete proof is required, and Lopez 2024 Appendix D.3 gives Bernoulli observation counterexamples. Therefore pi-VAE's printed proof does not establish the stated theorem; this is not a counterexample to its Poisson conclusion. A new proof using full Poisson count distributions and the count-to-intensity-law bridge, followed by a checked iVAE continuous-law argument, is a possible repair, not a completed composition. Appendix B states the implementation's Gaussian label prior and injective extended GIN architecture; official code audit remains pending.

**Lee & Gu, [Deep Discrete Encoders](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.01414), 2025, Theorems 1–3.** Hierarchical binary latent layers with conditionally independent observed coordinates can have Poisson conditional observations. Theorem 1 uses two pure children per latent plus a separate configuration-separation requirement; Theorem 2 instead gives generic graph and parameter identification for analytic families and links with known latent-layer sizes, with the measure-zero exceptional set defined in the coefficient space for a fixed true graph (alternative graphs remain allowed), two disjoint perfect matchings and at least one child in a third set for each latent at every layer. Target: within-model parameters and latent graph up to stated label/sign symmetries, not automatically cross-assay coupling. Theorem 3 gives conditional root-n consistency assuming identifiable model, bounded coefficients and nonsingular Fisher information. Estimator: layerwise spectral initialization then penalized stochastic approximation EM; small models can use penalized EM. Cross-assay matching of latent state weights would be a new composition, requiring same selected state weights, distinctness and consistent label/sign handling; testable within-assay marginal predictive fit is not proof of those assumptions. Structural condition, finite class assumption and depth remain unverified for these data. Code/proof inspection not yet complete.

**Yacobi et al. [Learning Shared Representations from Unpaired Data](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/429b76977aa377ed6dff3b91e9163b40-Paper-Conference.pdf), 2025.** SUE learns marginal spectral embeddings using unpaired observations but explicitly requires a small paired subset for cross-modal CCA alignment (Sections 1 and 4; Appendix E.8). No such pairs are permitted for training here. A marginal-only symmetry-breaking adaptation is a separate unproved method, not their fitted model. This is an observation-contract finding, not a general impossibility claim.

**Liu–Zhong MultiCOAP [2024 preprint §2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.10542), later 2026 journal version.** Theorem 1 claims model identifiability when covariates are independent of latent effects with positive-definite second moment; loading matrices satisfy stated ordered orthogonality and sign normalizations; p−1>q+q_s; and each study-specific loading column changes in some study. It models conditional Poisson counts with Gaussian shared and study-specific factors and residual variation; observations need not be paired across studies, but the same p features and shared loading matrix are used. Treating different assay feature spaces as studies therefore requires new observation modeling. The paper uses variational estimation and its exact population target and supplement proof remain under review. Potential viable use is comparison across genuinely common-feature studies if authorized metadata supplies those labels; predictive checks cannot prove the imposed latent decomposition.

**Xu et al., MuDo-CoM, [2026 Theorems 3.2–3.3 and 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.28330).** Unpaired domains with dependent latent vector z are generated by X^τ=A D^τ z, a common full-column-rank A and latent law, domain-diagonal scalings and positive-definite finite latent covariance. The sufficient variability assumption requires m=binomial(n+3,4) domains whose degree-four scaling-monomial matrix is full rank and nonzero scales. Identifies A up to permutation/scaling and source components up to those plus per-domain sign; a common-sign condition removes sign mismatch. Estimation minimizes covariance-matching loss; asymptotic recovery assumes consistent sample covariance and global minimization of a nonconvex objective. Two assay IDs do not satisfy the stated sufficient domain count for n≥2, nor do distinct assay feature maps and stochastic counts supply one shared noiseless A. A Poisson moment and common-feature-environment extension would need a new theorem; held-out covariance fit alone does not verify latent-domain invariance or global optimality.

**GLUE, [Cao & Gao 2022, Nature Biotechnology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-022-01284-4.pdf), Methods.** Designed for unpaired single-cell assays with different feature dimensions, count likelihoods including negative binomial, and a signed weighted cross-feature guidance graph. Learns feature/cell embeddings using variational data/graph reconstruction and adversarial distribution alignment; may reweight modality embeddings for composition mismatch. This is a candidate practical estimator only if the available guidance graph and data contract match, not an identified cross-assay joint or genomic DAG theorem; neither adversarial convergence nor good predictive fit proves latent orientation. The paper's integration-consistency score explicitly uses the guidance graph itself, so it is not independent validation of graph edges. A prespecified existing-data validation could report held-out within-assay count reconstruction and sensitivity to graph/weight choices; any authorized paired holdout must be sequestered for final evaluation. Official code and details require review; no run approved.

**Tang et al., [Network Analysis of Count Data from Mixed Populations](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.03665), Theorems 1–3.** A class-conditional Gaussian log-intensity with full covariance gives conditionally independent Poisson counts given the log-intensities, but correlated observed counts within class. Known per-cell scaling is assumed. Theorem 1 claims identifiability and positive Fisher information with bounded positive precision eigenvalues, iid positive bounded scaling, and bounded distinct component means; §2.2 explicitly states means and mixing weights are known throughout the theory. Theorem 3's support result also needs Fisher irrepresentability and tuning. Their practical VMPLN is variational, whereas theory is for exact lasso-penalized likelihood and does not automatically certify the algorithm. The recovered precision graph is undirected conditional dependence, not a directed causal network. It is a candidate class to replace independent Poisson components but extension to unknown weights and unpaired cross-assay alignment requires a separate proof and selection assumptions. Held-out count moments and residual dependence may falsify fit, not prove truth.

**Ben-Ari, Talmon & Shaham, [Unpaired Canonical Correlation Analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09530), 2026 preprint, §§4–5.** Theorem 1 equates the Frobenius-relaxed maximum-correlation pairing to quadratic assignment; Theorem 2 equates that relaxation and orthogonal criterion under a weak-majorization assumption. Their foundational setup assumes the two rows have a true permutation P* and, separately, that P* maximizes the CCA objective; no observed paired labels are required in the optimization. Algorithm whitens, constructs anchors, approximates quadratic assignment, then computes CCA. Neither theorem shows that independent biological cohorts have a true sample permutation or that the biological coupling maximizes CCA; approximate solver/global optimum and different sample sizes need further inspection. It is an alignment comparator, not a demonstrated cross-assay identification theorem.

**Zheng & Zhang, [Generalizing Nonlinear ICA Beyond Structural Sparsity](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.00866), NeurIPS 2023.** Theorem 3.1 is a distinct no-auxiliary, undercomplete nonlinear ICA result: mutually independent continuous sources, injective twice-differentiable decoder with full-column-rank Jacobian, row-support spanning condition, and for every source an intersection of decoder Jacobian row supports isolating it; learned decoder must have no larger support. It identifies single-view sources up to permutation and elementwise invertible transformations, not cross-assay alignment or discrete counts. The paper's Theorems 4.1(ii), 4.3(iii), 4.4(iii), and 4.2 by inheritance impose the same positive-measure, every exact non-cylinder set must change probability condition inherited from Kong. For atomless nontrivial product supports, delete a common null point from the entire support: resulting exact non-cylinder retains probability one in both domains. Thus those printed premises are inconsistent in that setting; repair and proof need separate work. This argument does not invalidate Theorem 3.1, nor all nonlinear ICA. Need inspect official estimator/code and proof before use.

**Benhamza, Clausel & Tami, [Identifiable Multimodal Causal Representation Learning under Partial Latent Sharing](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19135), 2026 preprint.** Theorem 4.1 states block recovery under a common continuous latent vector, per-modality injective smooth proper full-rank maps, and specified causal direction/conditional independences. Theorem 4.2 adds that each modality has another with disjoint sharing neighborhoods, and causal Jacobian mixing-density plus no-denser estimated graph conditions; it claims componentwise recovery. Two modalities sharing a latent cannot satisfy its neighborhood-disjoint condition. Their Wasserstein assignment uses Euclidean distances between concatenated latent sample-coordinate vectors; whether independent samples are permitted by implementation is unresolved, as are proof details. Deterministic continuous observation omits Poisson sampling, and structural properties are not verified. Code and proofs need audit; no fitted model authorized.

**Shrestha & Fu, [Diversified Distribution Matching](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.09671), ICLR 2024.** Continuous inverse domain maps and shared observed auxiliary traits u_i are assumed, with every corresponding conditional law carried by the same true map. Definition 4 requires every pair of disjoint connected nonempty open sets to be distinguished by at least one auxiliary conditional law; Lemma A.1 claims unique map under that condition and exact matching. This is a restricted open-set condition, so the earlier all-measurable null-point contradiction is not automatically applicable. Their Theorem 1 adds probabilistic event assumptions, and the adversarial estimator requires global optimization. Current assay labels alone do not provide shared cross-domain trait values or a deterministic map between noisy counts. A shared permitted biological covariate would need a separate count and conditional-law argument, and empirical conditional fit would not prove map correctness.

**Nguyen & Fu, [Diverse Influence Component Analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17040), NeurIPS 2025, Assumption 3.1 and Theorems 3.2–3.3.** For every latent s, rows ∇f_i(s) must lie in a weighted ℓ1 ball whose maximal-volume inscribed ellipsoid lies inside their convex hull; the hull's polar must meet the boundary of the ellipsoid's polar only at the weighted ℓ∞ ball vertices. A globally optimal Jacobian-volume criterion, universal encoder/decoder, smooth injective noiseless mixing, and this condition at every s yield a single permutation and coordinatewise invertible transformations, without latent independence or auxiliary u. Finite-SDI Theorem 3.3 requires finite SDI-satisfying points, sufficiently rich function classes, continuity and coverage plus optimality; it is not an unconditional noisy finite-sample count theorem. Geometry is latent and empirically unverifiable from raw counts alone. Potential Poisson intensity-operator composition and independent cross-assay alignment remain new proofs, and global optimization / exact fit remain substantive computational assumptions.

**DICA finite-sample premise needs scrutiny.** Theorem 3.3 condition 5, equation (11), literally bounds below the absolute difference of the scalar surrogate V(s;Θ) by a positive multiple of the Euclidean distance of every pair of parameter vectors. Such a map must be injective, impossible for a continuous scalar function on an open subset of a parameter space of dimension greater than one; neural networks also typically have parameter symmetries. Unless the theorem's parameter space is restricted to an appropriate lower-dimensional set or quotiented in a way satisfying the inequality, this creates a nonvacuity gap for its stated finite-sample claim. This observation does not refute Theorem 3.2 or show the finite-sample conclusion is false; independent mathematical review and the exact parameter domain are needed.

**Yan et al., [Counterfactual Generation with Identifiability Guarantees](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15309), NeurIPS 2023.** Thm 1 needs known content/style dimensions; smooth invertible noiseless decoder and inverse; support-spanning variation; each content-coordinate decoder Jacobian column has strictly more nonzero feature influences than every style coordinate; an alternative model exactly matches the observed distribution and globally minimizes total style Jacobian ℓ0 support. It identifies content only up to an invertible block map. Thm 2 adds that each content/style support pair neither contains the other and minimizes support intersections to identify the style block up to an invertible map. Conditional content/style dependence is permitted; no all-measurable-domain-variability premise occurs in these two results. Actual estimator MATTE uses a differentiable Jacobian ℓ1 surrogate, which does not certify the theorem's global ℓ0 optimum. Discrete noisy counts are outside the direct deterministic decoder model; Poisson rate-law composition and cross-assay coordinate alignment remain new proofs. Code https://github.com/hanqi-qi/Matte.git located, not audited.

**scConCRL, [Dong, Li & Wang, IJCAI 2025](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/0822.pdf).** Published main text models same-gene scRNA observations with a conditional Gaussian-source SEM, known domain labels, DAG penalty and gene-relationship guidance; it refers its identification foundation to Appendix A, which was not found in the retrieved author-repository tree. Root retrieved source-only `sources/scconcrl_root` commit `85bef06241331d8d939e24f448c114f19f4dd28f`; `scConCRL/model.py` `calculate_loss` uses squared reconstruction, Gaussian KL, DAG/L1/L2 and adversarial/domain terms, not a Poisson/NB likelihood in that path. Distinct assay feature maps require model changes. The paper's empirical GRN predictions and DAG regularizer do not by themselves establish identifiable count-data causal structure. Proof and full code audit remain open; no code executed.

**Moran & Krishnan, [Nonlinear multi-study sparse factor analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18128), 2026 preprint v2, count-native continuous candidate.** Thm 2 is *single-study NB*: known fixed library depths, two pure anchor features per continuous factor with proportional strictly monotone conditional means, non-anchor nonparallelity, covariance and conditional-latent/functional nondegeneracy. It claims anchors and dimension, factor distribution up to coordinatewise transforms and permutation, support and dispersions. Appendix A first identifies anchor dispersion from covariance, then uses Hausdorff moment determinacy on a transformed known-shape NB mixing law. Thm 3's multi-study canonical support intersection is stated for Gaussian observations, not NB; extending it to NB and across different feature universes is unproved. Remark 3 says the proposed NB-MSSVAE estimates library size as latent while Thm 2 assumes it known/fixed, so that estimator is outside the stated depth theorem. Its anchors and faithfulness are unverified, raw cell totals are not automatically known exogenous depths, and population-law identification is not recovered cell states. Literature/supplement proof and available code require independent audit. Potential existing-data checks: prespecified normalized cross-feature anchor-correlation diagnostics and count predictive fit under an approved model, neither capable of establishing true factor structure or cross-assay mapping.
