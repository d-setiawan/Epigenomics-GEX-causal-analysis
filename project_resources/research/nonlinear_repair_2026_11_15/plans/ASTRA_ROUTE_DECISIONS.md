# Astra route decisions before C01 execution

Date: 2026-11-15. This records scientific scope before any new fitted outcome.

The reviewed continuous repair D replaces the lossy Poisson-to-Bernoulli proof route with full-count Laplace injection and Radon–Nikodym contrasts on the intensity support. It is a substantive positive nonlinear count theorem, with an explicit normal-location/exponential-decoder example and an independent proof review. It identifies an affine equivalence class of sufficient statistics under observed auxiliary modulation and common injective decoder assumptions. The permitted assay labels do not supply these assumptions: heterogeneous assay coordinates need not share a decoder, and invariant shared content cannot simultaneously supply a full-rank content-modulation contrast. We therefore do not fit pi-VAE to assay IDs and call that a validation of D.

Repair C gives a separate nonvacuous continuous content/style model with a known inverse nonlinear link, common transformed coordinates and full style mean contrasts. It identifies a content quotient law and its count posterior. Those common-coordinate domains are absent in the current three-assay inputs. Repair A illustrates a valid restricted encoder result but is not enforceable from these observations alone. None is advertised as restoring the unrestricted all-measurable variability theorem.

C01 instead tests the finite shared-state route B on the actual permitted counts. Multivariate Poisson mixture injection identifies each assay's finite intensity atoms; distinct exactly common weights would align the atoms. This accommodates arbitrary differing assay response vectors and feature dimensions. It does not require a shared gene/peak loading map or unknown paired cells. Those advantages make it the executable route here, while its product emissions, finite states, common population weights and separability remain strong assumptions. Independent fits, calibration selection, bootstrap profile/weight checks, ambiguity enumeration, a column-permutation control and negative-binomial sensitivity are prescribed before execution. Assay-local QC can violate common weights; depth can dominate states. Acceptance of a compatibility diagnostic will not prove the assumptions.

Across-assay conditional independence is an additional joint-completion restriction, assessed only after prediction sealing against the existing paired reference. A failure of that prediction cannot refute count-channel injection; conversely a good prediction cannot identify a causal graph or establish the continuous auxiliary theorem. Existing simulator observations provide application checks under misspecification, not newly generated theorem-satisfying benchmark worlds. Deterministic algebra/formal fixtures separately check tractable proof steps.

Other reviewed count routes are retained in the literature dispositions, including shared-feature multi-study factor models, anchor/pure-child discrete models, nonlinear mixture decoders and guidance-based integration. A route requiring unavailable pairing, auxiliary labels, common feature coordinates, certified anchors, temporal dependence or a known technical-noise structure is not silently supplied those inputs. The literature review remains open while source and citation-family checks finish; C01 is not evidence that every alternative has been exhausted.

Independent implementation replay and the final applicability audit remain required after execution. No hidden state or paired value has been used to choose this route, the model grid or its stopping rules.
