**Earlier multivariate Poisson predecessor.** Chaussard, Bonnet, Gassiat and Le Corff, [PLN-Tree, §4 Lemma 1 and Appendix §9.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17361), explicitly state positive multivariate rate-law identification from a joint Poisson count law. The appendix supplies its own generating-function and compact-support moment proof, then invokes the same argument in several coordinates. Its printed scalar proof uses the generating function for all real t without addressing integrability; the valid bounded-domain argument and the multivariate extension require care, so cite it as an explicit predecessor, not a fully audited substitute for our proof. Our independently reviewed proof also allows zero rates. Its Theorem 1 instead assumes an observed count hierarchy with Poisson roots and multinomial children summing to their parent; this specific observed hierarchy is absent from the current assay vectors.

**PWA–GMM count composition candidate.** With known coordinatewise exponential rate link, let Λ=exp(f(Z)) and X_j|Λ independently Poisson(Λ_j). Our Poisson law bridge and the invertible log identify the distribution of f(Z); applying [Beyond ICA, Theorems 5 and 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23182) is our composition. Their source assumes irredundant positive-weight full-covariance Gaussian mixture Z, reduced square f:R^d→R^d with invertible affine branches and full-dimensional polyhedral domains, plus branch/component parameter injectivity on both comparisons. Theorem 5 adds a truth-side simple boundary and recovers mixture law up to a global affine map; Theorem 8 adds universal simple boundaries and trivial mixture symmetry and identifies f under that affine map. Pointwise latent inversion additionally requires f globally injective. This square model has d observed features and d latent coordinates, not automatic dimension reduction; it supplies no cross-assay coupling or causal orientation. The exponential link solves positivity but is an extra known measurement model. Whether these additional assumptions fit the counts, and the actual finite-sample estimator/code, remain open; the derivation is not an implementation recommendation.

**DCRL author code.** Code-only inspection is recorded in SEARCH_LOG and `sources/dcrl_root/retrieval_manifest.json`. A small-K within-assay predictive study is a plausible candidate under its stated assumptions; author code uses additive nonnegative Poisson rates, no observed depth offset, a row-order-sensitive initializer requiring J≥3K, and may fail on singular inverses. K=1 is outside the paper's K≥2 theorem. The Q condition can be checked as a certificate for a *fitted support*, not verified as a biological truth. The separately approved C02 author-code application has now run and passed independent numerical review; its [results](experiments/C02/RESULTS.md) preserve failures and nonconvergence.

## Continuation page 2: distinct newly surfaced routes (2026-11-15)

- **Garg, Stettler, Schein and von Kügelgen, 2026**, [Discrete Causal Representations from Heterogeneous Domains](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06288), §§2–4: multi-environment finite mixtures with invariant same-coordinate measurement components, discrete latent causal variables, sparse unknown soft mechanism shifts, KL-detectability prior; their implemented measurement model is additive Gaussian and inference uses sequential Monte Carlo. [Official repository](https://github.com/agarg7/discrete-bayesian-crl) is advertised by the paper. This is a distinct multi-domain estimator, but different assays do not establish invariant common observed coordinates or components; adapting to counts needs a new measurement likelihood and corresponding justification. The paper emphasizes posterior inference rather than claiming its mixture model alone identifies latent causal structure (§2.3, §8). A common-coordinate batch application is conceivable under additional assumptions, not demonstrated here.
- **Feldman and Gu, 2026**, [Identifiable Bayesian Deep Generative Copulas](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27523), Theorem 1 and Assumptions 1–3: the *copula law* identifies canonical parameters up to within-layer latent permutation, assuming known layer widths, positive configurations, oriented nonzero loading columns, two distinct pure children per latent at each layer, and separation of all latent configurations by remaining children. Continuous-margin exact rank-likelihood consistency differs from tied-count extended-rank generalized-posterior concentration, which requires the extra uniform contrast condition S.2, stronger than coarsened-rank identification. Tied count observations alone do not automatically identify the underlying copula. This is a plausible within-assay dependence estimator with additional model checks, not a cross-assay alignment result; code and practical suitability for these panels are not audited.
- **Song, Park and Park, 2026**, [Discrete Score Matching Enables Causal Discovery from Count Data](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.39326): DISCO estimates score curvature using discrete diffusion for a jointly observed count-feature DAG under semiparametric GLM conditional structure, canonical-parameter nonlinearity and stated regularity. The paper reports scaling to 1,000 nodes on a GPU. It is an adjacent observed-feature causal objective; it does not identify shared states or link unpaired assays. Source and computational conditions are not independently reproduced here.
- **Gao, Cai and Hara, PT-SEM**, §4 and Appendix A.3.5: exact DP-BIC evaluates d·2^(d−1) parent sets and has stated O((d^4+Nd²)2^d) time and O(d²2^d) space; d=30 already entails 16,106,127,360 local parent sets, d=300 approximately 3.06×10^92. Paper Assumption 1(c) says p(x|z) need not be invariant across environments, but Appendix F writes one common additive-noise decoder p_epsilon(x-f(z)); any use of the cited proof requires its actual common-kernel premise, not just the broad model assertion.

**Zhang–Kileel feasibility basis.** With J features, N cells and r≤4, choose moment orders d₁=3 and d₂=2 with positive τ₂ and τ₃. For J=30 or 300, the stated rank condition reduces to r≤min{floor((J−1)/2),binom(J,2)}, satisfied numerically. This does not establish genericity, conditional independence, a shared class population or class separation in current data. Algorithm 1 alternates rowwise mean least squares and a simplex-constrained quadratic weight step; simplex permits zero weights and mean updates are real-valued and divide by weights, so a raw finite fit can produce invalid Poisson rates or unstable zero-weight updates. With fixed order the authors give O(J N r+J r³) flops per mean-update sweep and O(r(J+N)) working memory, not measured runtime. The [source audit](reviews/MEITD_SOURCE_AUDIT.md) records implementation caveats. The [approved C03 plan](plans/C03_MEITD_VALIDATION.md) specifies the additional product-Poisson predictive model and invalid-fit handling; its implementation has passed independent pre-fit review.

For practical context, their improved Algorithm 6 uses a warm-up with one temporarily dropped moment, random row blocks, means restricted to the data cube and positive lower bounds on weights, then ordinary ALS plus Anderson acceleration. Section 6 reports standardizing each coordinate, τᵢ=(J−i)!/J!, first four orders, and both relative parameter changes below 10⁻⁴. They include Poisson-mixture examples (J=10 and a J=1024 imaging example), not these data. The pinned implementation clips every ordinary mean update to the observed data cube, including main stage; Anderson acceleration can extrapolate outside it, so final positivity after reversing standardization still needs checking. This differs from the paper's warmup-only description. Baseline local-convergence theorem does not automatically certify the improved heuristic or finite sample performance.

Their example identifies a never-coobserved edge using overlapping sets. For two fully disjoint sets, arbitrary disconnected within-block graphs and those same graphs plus one cross-block edge have identical within-block projections, so cross edges are not generally identifiable from these projections. This is an undirected observed-feature graph target, not latent-state alignment; source estimator not run.

**Lachapelle binary-assay implication (our inference).** Under the static T=1 specialization with one binary auxiliary taking exactly the two assay labels, at each fixed latent z there is only one independent score-difference vector: reversing the contrast negates it. Assumption 7 can therefore span the coordinate subspace of the auxiliary's affected latent components only when that subspace has dimension at most one. More observed levels, auxiliary coordinates or histories could change that rank calculation; the two-label special case does not support a generic claim that multiple sparse latent shifts are identifiable. The rate-law composition itself remains to be independently proved and reviewed.

For the actual three assay labels RNA, ac and me, model one categorical auxiliary with three levels and T=1: relative to any reference, at most two independent score-contrast vectors exist at a fixed z. The same source span assumption can then hold only if that auxiliary affects at most two latent coordinates. This rank bound concerns the theorem's sufficient-influence premise, not a blanket impossibility of higher-dimensional latent blocks or all other identification approaches; it also does not establish the other premises. More independent environments, actions or histories change the calculation.

Encoding matters: the three-label bound above uses one scalar categorical auxiliary. With a three-coordinate one-hot auxiliary, changing just one coordinate while fixing the others generally leaves the one-hot support, so the paper's admissible coordinatewise comparison condition needs separate verification; one cannot assume three freely varying auxiliary coordinates.

**Manring–Huang 2026: analytic nonlinear ICA with kinked source densities.** [v2 primary Theorem 1, Lemma 1 and Assumptions 1–2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.21926), §§3–4 and Appendix A: known common scalar source density ρ is iid across coordinates, has connected support and log density partitioned into finitely many analytic pieces with at least one breakpoint, nonzero one-sided derivative jumps, positive breakpoint gaps, and the paper's odd/even central analytic-continuation condition. For an analytic diffeomorphism h carrying ρ^⊗k to itself, Theorem 1 claims identity up to signed permutation and translation; with fixed known ρ, only transformations actually preserving ρ are admissible. Arbitrary separate scale changes are not an ambiguity in the fixed-prior theorem. Lemma 1 extends the comparison to analytically invertible generators with exact equality of observation laws and (when used) a known common additive-noise kernel whose characteristic function vanishes only on a null set. The authors propose analytic normalizing flows/VAEs; paper §8 says code will be released upon acceptance, so no faithful author-code audit or count estimator is established. Their own §4.3 warns practical recovery may fail for compact-support distributions despite the claimed population result.

**Independent counterexample:** the separate skeptical review finds the general Theorem 1 false as stated: for F(x)=x+a log cosh(x), 0<a<1, the density ρ(x)=0.5 exp(−|F(x)|) F′(x) meets its stated Assumption 2, yet the analytic diffeomorphism h(x)=F⁻¹(−F(x)) preserves ρ and has h″(0)=−2a, so is not affine. The coordinatewise map extends this to iid vectors. Appendix A’s odd-breakpoint reversal assertion (line 352 in v2 HTML) is the relevant proof step. This componentwise countermodel does not refute the weaker target up to arbitrary componentwise reparameterization. Exact Laplace admits the separate positive proof below; do not infer that all analytic nonlinear ICA or the authors’ practical algorithm fails.

A **separately proved restricted population composition** takes S with iid exact standard Laplace coordinates and strictly positive rate Λ=exp(f(S)), where f is a global real-analytic diffeomorphism R^k→R^k and X|Λ has independent Poisson coordinates. The [self-contained restricted proof](reviews/KINKS_SMOOTHNESS_COUNTEREXAMPLE.md) and [independent correspondence review](reviews/KINKS_INDEPENDENT_CORRESPONDENCE_REVIEW.md) establish analytic Laplace-preserver rigidity by kink-hyperplane matching and analytic continuation, without relying on the invalid general-prior reversal step. This class is nonempty (f=identity). Different assays retain independent signed permutations, so this does not align modalities, identify individual noisy-cell sources or recover a causal graph.
