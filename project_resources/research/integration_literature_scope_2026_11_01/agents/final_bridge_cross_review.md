# Cross-review of the literature synthesis: paired information and sampling

Reviewed `REVIEW.md` on 2026-11-01, SHA256 `26be6e5c9c02bc801bb2bd45e8803b3953bfb94ba14b30da63c348f8ae516291`. Reviewer: `scmtr_data_pilot`. This review focuses on paired/bridge input contracts and benchmark interpretation. It does not independently certify all 64 extractions or the central search counts.

**Scoped approval: no blocking error found.** The draft correctly distinguishes independently sampled disjoint assay pools from paired training, partially observed paired data, and shuffled views of a common finite sample. It does not convert integration success or an empirical failure into a population identification theorem. Two nonblocking wording improvements were sent to the lead:

1. At line 129, replace the ambiguous scMoMaT phrase “shared measured entities” with an explicit distinction between genuinely shared cell measurements and common feature identities. Same-cell observations can supply joint information. Shared feature identities and annotation-derived pseudo-modalities alone do not. The existing subsequent caveat is correct, but this clarification would prevent the section heading from suggesting that all scMoMaT modes escape the theorem. Evidence: framework entity connectivity and Methods, “Calculating pseudo-count matrices.” [scMoMaT](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-36066-2).
2. At line 139, state that the motivating pseudobulk distortion examples use **RNA–ATAC**, while RNA–histone data form another arm of the component benchmark. Both facts in the draft are true, but the current conjunction could suggest the depicted peak–gene failures occurred in the histone arm. Evidence: Fig. 1f–g and “A motivating example” versus the RNA–histone evaluation. [Naqing, Yuan and Duren](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13059-026-04071-5).

The following substantive claims were checked:

- Lines 125–129 correctly classify the published paired or bridge regimes. Missing-modality inference at deployment does not erase paired training information. The classifications agree with the 17 primary-paper extractions in `bridge_methods.json`; JAMIE and scMoMaT remain mode-dependent. No claim that these methods cannot estimate useful cross-assay relationships is made.
- Line 131's MultiVI sampling distinction is directly supported by its primary paper. In its artificial-unpairing experiment, selected cells yield both an RNA-only row and an ATAC-only row, while other cells retain both measurements. Its independent-study demonstration also includes a jointly measured subset: 13% of the combined cells. Thus neither example supplies a counterexample to a theorem premised on independent disjoint assay pools without a paired bridge. This does not establish a theorem about every possible MultiVI configuration. Evidence: Results, “MultiVI integrates paired and unpaired samples” and “Integration of independent studies.” [MultiVI](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-023-01909-9).
- Lines 133–139 appropriately separate benchmark endpoints from general causal-graph guarantees. The Registered Report explicitly separates seven tasks. SCMBench's Methods describe simulations for batch correction and cell clustering; its reported evaluation targets do not become exact causal graph recovery merely because the generator contains regulatory structure. [Multitask Registered Report](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-025-02856-3), [SCMBench](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13342640/).
- The component benchmark explicitly treats paired RNA–histone data as unpaired, and its imbalance analysis subsamples one view while preserving the underlying RNA–ATAC pairings. This confirms the value of paired evaluator references without making the original sample draw a product of independent assay laws. Its reported agreement/distortion is not interventional ground truth. [Component benchmark](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13059-026-04071-5).
- Lines 5–11, 71–79 and 143–153 preserve the appropriate limits: a broad-class nonidentification result, possible positive restricted models, and no claim that every dataset or every recovered edge must fail. The recommendation does not silently add paired training, labels or other forbidden observations to the project.
