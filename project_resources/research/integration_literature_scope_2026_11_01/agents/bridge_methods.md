# Paired, bridge and factor methods: boundaries of the unpaired result

This contribution reviews 17 selected primary papers. It is a bounded scoping review, not an exhaustive survey. The central OpenAlex searches preceded the targeted checks recorded in `bridge_search_records.json` and its addendum. Sixteen records include inspected Methods; the Seurat v5 bridge record is deliberately marked `abstract`, supplemented by primary indexed Results and figure descriptions because the full Methods could not be retrieved reliably. `bridge_methods.json` contains the individual input contracts, evidence locations and caveats.

The decisive distinction is the information available during **training**, including pretrained models and reference datasets. A method may accept an unpaired query while its training data contain paired cells. Such a method can use joint information that is absent from the strict-disjoint theorem. Conversely, a computed gene-activity matrix or inferred correspondence does not constitute a new measured modality.

The right column is our application of the information-based theorem, not a claim made by each original paper. “Outside” means that the theorem cannot establish an impossibility result for that regime. It does not mean that an embedding or reconstruction objective proves causal identification.

Several boundaries matter especially for this project. Paired references can support cross-modality prediction; they need not recover every cell-specific residual, and transfer to another population requires additional assumptions. Joint-distribution estimation also remains distinct from identifying causal directions. Paired observational data can leave Markov-equivalent or confounded causal explanations unresolved.

Missing-data support does not erase this distinction. A factor model with shared sample indices can exploit observed overlap. If all assay blocks are disconnected, merely allowing missing values does not reveal their unknown joint law. A low-rank or independent-noise assumption may select a particular model, but causal identification then needs a separate argument within that restricted model class. This is a reviewer inference about the observation pattern, not a negative empirical result for MOFA or MOFA+.

Mowgli is a useful safeguard against reasoning from an objective's name: its OT term reconstructs within-modality feature distributions, while identified same-cell columns connect the modalities. scMoMaT illustrates the complementary boundary: annotation-derived pseudo-counts can aid alignment, but do not turn unpaired cells into measured pairs. Its cluster-specific markers are a different target from direct regulatory edges. The corresponding primary methods specify these inputs and objectives. [Mowgli](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-43019-2), [scMoMaT](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-36066-2).

Benchmark construction also needs attention. MultiVI's major artificial-unpairing experiment splits selected originally paired cells into two unimodal observations and retains a paired subset. This differs from independent disjoint sampling in both its remaining pairs and the common original cells behind the shuffled views. Performance there does not establish universal recovery under the strict-disjoint experiment. [MultiVI](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10406609/).

The defensible literature conclusion is narrow: the inspected paired and bridge methods are legitimate positive boundaries, while their reported integration, prediction and marker targets do not by themselves demonstrate general causal graph recovery. Applying the theorem to a strictly disjoint mode requires checking its entire information contract. It would be incorrect to claim that these papers prove integration cannot recover any relationships, or to treat all their results as tests of the same unpaired experiment.
