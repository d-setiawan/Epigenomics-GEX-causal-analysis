## Material correction: finite mixtures and count blocks

[Allman et al.](https://www.cs.uaf.edu/~jrhodes/papers/Latent.pdf), Section 7, Theorem 8 treats the univariate-coordinate product model (4). **Theorem 9**, immediately following it, is the separately stated extension to vector-valued blocks in model (5), not Theorem 8 itself. The section states absolute-continuity conditions on its probability measures. The ledger's phrases “Theorem 8 … variables (or blocks)” and “each assay separately satisfies Allman T8 using its own three … feature blocks” conflate these statements and do not establish that the cited continuous block theorem directly covers discrete integer count blocks. A discrete latent-class/Kruskal theorem with the actual support and rank hypotheses, or a fully justified reduction, is needed before making that application. This is a theorem-attribution and applicability gap, not a refutation of the proposed mixture idea.

The same primary paper supplies the needed **discrete** route: Theorem 1 and Corollary 2 (printed p. 17, PDF p. 17) treat a finite three-variate latent-class model. With positive class weights, its condition is I₁+I₂+I₃ ≥ 2r+2 for the Kruskal ranks of the conditional-probability matrices. In the concrete Bernoulli example with r=2, three 2×2 matrices of full rank have Kruskal ranks 2 each; 6=2r+2. The corollary therefore identifies the class probabilities and conditional matrices up to simultaneous class permutation. Stochastic normalization removes the tensor row-rescaling ambiguity. This verifies that finite example under its stated conditional-independence and rank premises; it does not automatically extend the finite-state theorem to unrestricted count vectors. For finite categorical blocks the same corollary applies with appropriate category sizes and Kruskal ranks. A general composition may instead explicitly assume within-assay identifiability and specify its applicable theorem separately.

Conditional on a valid within-assay identification theorem for the relevant distribution class, the composed alignment argument is mathematically sound: positive, pairwise distinct equal class weights determine a unique matching of the identified class permutations. For three or more assays a common selected-population class law is necessary; a product cross-assay joint further requires mutual conditional independence across assays. The observed class-mixture fits do not verify either premise. Repeated weights leave potential permutations and, absent cross-assay conditional independence, residual couplings remain free. The composition is the program's derivation, not Allman's stated theorem. P07 correctly treats this as conditional and not ready for fitting, but must cite the appropriate theorem and resolve discrete-data applicability.

## Primary checks of key results

* [Sturma et al.](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/6c8985579293e0209bdaa4f21bb1d237-Paper-Conference.pdf), Theorem 3.1 (p. 6) takes m≥2 and C1/C2, identifies B and the source law up to signed block permutations, and explicitly states the resulting joint is identified. Theorem 4.4 (p. 8) assumes the recovered B representation plus C3/C4; its output is the shared-latent adjacency matrix up to its specified signed permutation, with an additional positive-loading condition eliminating sign ambiguity. This is not a named genomic graph. The ledger and P02 preserve the critical distinction. C1 includes non-Gaussianity; it should remain explicit wherever the theorem assumptions are summarized.
* [Timilsina et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.19422), Assumption 1 and Theorems 1–3: Theorem 1 first removes private components under modality variability and rank, and its separate alternative conditions give a common transformed content representation. Theorem 2 additionally imposes homogeneous mixing and a common encoder with Assumption 2. Theorem 3 explicitly imposes at least d_C **paired** samples in equality constraints. The corrected ledger and P08 properly exclude this third result from unpaired fitting. Population optimality does not certify optimization or the count channel. Reference 8 explicitly cites Sturma, verifying this forward citation; no full code audit was performed here.
* [Khemakhem et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v108/khemakhem20a/khemakhem20a.pdf), Theorems 1–3 (pp. 5–6), confirms the auxiliary-conditioned factorial exponential-family model, injectivity, characteristic-function and auxiliary-variation rank conditions, and the difference between invertible sufficient-statistic mixing in T1 and block-permutation equivalence under additional T2/T3 restrictions. This does not mean any auxiliary label is valid or that the project's Poisson sequencing channel is covered by the additive model. The paper also discusses discrete observations elsewhere; the judgment here concerns the cited theorem's model and is not a universal rejection of all discrete iVAE extensions.
* [Balabdaoui et al.](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume22/20-689/20-689.pdf), Theorem 1 (p. 10), gives the ledger's integrated L1 stochastic rate under A0–A5 and endpoint tightness, automatically established at interior quantiles. The known independent structural-noise distribution and monotonicity are critical. [Slawski and Sen](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume25/22-0058/22-0058.pdf), Theorem 8 (p. 16), independently checks the iid unlinked sample, absolute continuity, convex-gradient assumptions and Gaussian independent noise, and its stated high-probability empirical error bound. Proposition 2 has the different permuted-sample design. Neither result identifies an unknown biological direction solely from count marginals.
* **Poisson count derivation:** for fixed known d>0 and Λ≥0, conditional Poisson sampling gives G_C(z)=E exp(−d(1−z)Λ) for 0≤z≤1. Equality of count laws yields equality of Laplace transforms on an interval; uniqueness requires the analytic continuation/uniqueness property of Laplace transforms, not merely knowing one transform value. Coordinatewise independent Poisson counts similarly expose the multivariate transform on a nonempty open rectangle. This is a mathematical derivation under an exact known channel, not a newly inspected primary theorem. The historical Teicher full proof and claims for NB, masking, selection, endogenous depth, or unknown channels were not independently verified in this review. Intensities do not supply a cross-assay coupling.
* [Syrota et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.11460), Proposition 3.1, expresses competing alignments through metric-measure automorphisms and identifies the alignment when that group is trivial. HTML extraction omits hypothesis labels; the rendered complete assumptions remain unresolved, so P10's provisional status is appropriate. No claim follows for count data or local optimizer output.
