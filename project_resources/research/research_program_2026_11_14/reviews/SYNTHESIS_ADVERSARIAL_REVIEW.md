## Addendum: Shrestha–Fu nonlinear content/style, 2026-11-14

Independently inspected the [primary paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.03755), Section 2 Eq. (1), Section 3 Assumptions 3.1–3.2, Problems (2)/(4), Theorems 3.3–3.4, and Appendices C/D. P11 correctly distinguishes the paper's common differentiable bijection and common observed coordinate space from the heterogeneous noisy count assays. Theorem 3.3 concerns a differentiable injective population solution of exact content-law matching and within-domain content/style independence with known block dimensions; its claimed conclusion is a common injective transformation for each block, not componentwise identification or a genomic graph. Its Assumption 3.1 literally makes content and all domain-style blocks mutually independent, not merely independence within each content/style pair. Theorem 3.4 adds selected dimensions no smaller than the true ones, positive finite latent densities, and an exact expected style ℓ0 objective under the same constraints. A generic finite GAN/ℓ1 fit is not the stated population optimum, and no consistency or success guarantee follows merely from these theorems. P11's decision against an immediate count fit is justified by these substantive mismatches, not by a blanket rejection of nonlinear identification.

**Material theorem-premise concern requiring reconciliation:** as literally written, Assumption 3.2 quantifies over *every measurable* A⊆C×S of positive probability in every domain that is not exactly B×S, and requires a pair of domain probabilities to differ. Under the continuous latent densities and nontrivial open content/style supports used in Theorem 3.4, fix one point z₀∈C×S and choose A=(C×S)\{z₀}. This is measurable and is not exactly B×S, yet every absolutely continuous domain law assigns it probability 1. No domain pair can satisfy the required strict inequality. Thus the stated assumption appears inconsistent in this continuous setting, absent a qualification such as identifying sets modulo common null sets. Such a qualification is **not** itself a proved repair: one must examine whether a corrected variability assumption is satisfiable and sufficient for the proof. This review flags a literal-statement issue; it does not claim a counterexample to a corrected theorem, nor deny that other nonlinear identification results can hold. Until an author clarification or rigorous correction is available, describe 3.3/3.4 as the authors' *claimed conditional results with unresolved assumption consistency*, not an established nonvacuous positive model class or a confirmed route for these assays.

Reconcile this caveat in the theorem ledger, P11, synthesis and cycle record before the final audit. The paper's source code and citation follow-up are separate reviews; no code was executed or data generated for this check.
