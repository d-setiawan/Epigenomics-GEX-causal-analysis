## Open evidence and distinctions

The eight-family map is a coverage scaffold, not saturated review. Classic primary linear ICA, original optimal transport theorem, historical mixed-Poisson theorem, anchor-specific results, count-model supplements and actual repository code require more review. All positive results require the original theorem's full conditions, not the abbreviated rows above. Population identification, estimating an objective, estimator consistency, and finite-sample recovery are distinct. No new biological assays, training pairs, state labels, interventions or synthetic worlds were introduced.

## Additional primary results and composed candidate (2026-11-14)

**Comon 1994, Theorem 11 and Corollary 13**, [primary scan, pp. 294–295](https://fileserver-az.core.ac.uk/download/pdf/52776748.pdf): for nondegenerate independent components with at most one Gaussian, an orthogonal transformation preserves component independence only if it is a permutation times a diagonal matrix. Corollary 13 is a noiseless linear ICA identifiability statement; general scale/permutation ambiguities remain without normalization. This concerns the full joint of coordinates within a view and population distributions, not matched components across unpaired assays or latent DAGs. At-most-one-Gaussian, invertibility and independence are model assumptions; some observed fit implications can be rejected, but finite sample normality tests cannot prove them. No reproduction.

**Hyvärinen and Pajunen 1999, Sections 2–3**, [author primary manuscript](https://www.cs.helsinki.fi/u/ahyvarin/papers/NN99.pdf): unrestricted nonlinear mixing admits infinitely many nontrivially different independent-component solutions; known pointwise transformation indeterminacy is not the only ambiguity. Section 3 describes restricted two-dimensional conformal cases rather than a universal impossibility. This is a population nonuniqueness result and does not refute the auxiliary-variable or multi-view positive theorems. A good independent-component fit cannot select among equivalent nonlinear descriptions.

**Timilsina, Shrestha and Fu 2024, Theorems 1–3**, [primary paper with appendices B–D and H](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.19422): for two unpaired distributions of linear mixtures of common content c and modality-specific components, full-column-rank mixing, full-rank content second moment, and their exact modality variability Assumption 1, any population solution of constrained distribution matching in Eq. 6 removes private components: Q^(q)x^(q)=Θ^(q)c. Alignment of Θ matrices requires additional anti-symmetry assumptions (Theorem 1 cases a/b); distribution matching by itself does not align latent coordinates. Theorem 2 replaces those cases with the strong homogeneous mixing and common encoder constraint plus its own variability Assumption 2. Theorem 3 instead requires at least d_C paired training samples, Assumption 1, full-column-rank mixing, and absolutely continuous content; it is incompatible with this project’s unpaired fitting contract. Targets are content representations up to invertible linear transformation, not componentwise causal variables or a feature-level graph. Full-rank checks on observed covariances may reject implications, but latent modality variability and correspondence are generally untestable from separate views. Observed biological counts and their measurement kernels are not the paper's noiseless linear input model. The [focused official code audit](TIMILSINA_CODE_AUDIT.md) inspects synthetic and single-cell source without execution. The shipped single-cell command uses paired supervision, and its diagnostic evaluation path warrants caution; other source paths and actual execution remain unaudited. Timilsina's reference [8] actually cites Sturma (their reference gives incorrect year; primary publication is 2023), providing a verified forward citation.

**Allman, Matias and Rhodes 2009**, [primary paper](https://www.cs.uaf.edu/~jrhodes/papers/Latent.pdf): distinguish the finite-state **Theorem 1 / Corollary 2** from the continuous scalar **Theorem 8** and continuous block **Theorem 9**. The finite three-block latent-class model is identifiable up to common class permutation with positive weights and Kruskal-rank sum at least 2r+2. In the two-class Bernoulli example each conditional 2×2 matrix has rank 2, so 2+2+2=6=2r+2. Probability normalization resolves tensor scaling. Continuous results require their stated absolute-continuity and class-conditional measure-independence conditions; they must not be applied unqualified to integer count blocks. Corollary 11 concerns its stated observed-margin rank conditions. All results require a complete within-cell joint across the feature blocks, not three disconnected assay marginals. A valid within-assay block model may supply such a joint, but latent conditional independence and rank remain premises. These are population results, not a causal-graph theorem. The [independent review](../reviews/LITERATURE_ADVERSARIAL_REVIEW.md) records the material correction to the original draft.

**Composed finite-mixture route (our derivation, not a theorem attributed to Allman):** Suppose each assay separately has an identified finite mixture, using a theorem valid for its observation family (for finite categorical blocks, the Kruskal condition in Allman Corollary 2), with its own jointly observed conditionally independent feature blocks, with the same known number r of shared classes, the same strictly positive class probabilities after selection, and all class weights pairwise distinct. The identified multiset of weights is then identical in every assay and uniquely matches the class permutations. Thus the class-conditional *within-assay* distributions are matched by shared weights. With the additional assumption that assay blocks are independent conditional on class, their product reconstructs the cross-assay observational joint. Without that extra assumption, class matching identifies class-associated feature profiles but not the residual cross-assay joint. Repeated weights permit class-label permutations; finite-sample near ties create instability. Correct common classes, common selected-population probabilities, and conditional cross-assay independence are untestable from unpaired marginals alone. Assay-specific QC/selection may break the common weights even when preselection weights agree, and continuous factors may invalidate the finite-mixture model. Within-assay observed rank and fit can provide partial diagnostics, not validation of the composed assumptions. This is a conditional positive candidate for Astra's plan; no fitting or generation has been performed.

**Anandkumar et al. 2014, Theorem 3.6**, [primary JMLR pp. 2784–85](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume15/anandkumar14b/anandkumar14b.pdf): under a discrete hidden class and three jointly observed views conditionally independent given it, with linearly independent conditional mean vectors in each view, cross-moments transform into a symmetric second/third moment decomposition. Theorem 3.6 establishes the moment identities; the later tensor-decomposition results supply recovery under additional requirements. It must not be described as itself a finite-sample guarantee or as requiring just three disconnected marginals. Theorem 3.1 concerns a single-topic exchangeable model, not generic multi-view recovery. Within-assay feature groupings could supply joint blocks, subject to substantive conditional independence and rank.

**Arora et al. 2013, Theorem 4.3**, [primary paper pp. 5–6](https://people.csail.mit.edu/dsontag/papers/AroraEtAl_icml13.pdf): for noisy points around a γ-robust simplex whose K vertices occur among the clean points, ε<γ³/(20K), an algorithm returns K points O(ε/γ)-covering the vertices. It is a deterministic finite-perturbation geometric statement; application to topic recovery uses its model and anchor-word/separability construction. A feature annotation or candidate regulatory edge is not evidence of an exclusive anchor, nor does a within-assay anchor imply a shared class across assays. Robustness and separability may be examined empirically but latent exclusivity cannot generally be proved from finite counts. This does not identify causal directions.

**Brenier 1991, Theorems 1.1–1.2**, [primary paper](https://www.math.toronto.edu/~mccann/assignments/477/Brenier91.pdf): under the stated measure-space, integrability and nondegeneracy assumptions there is a unique convex-gradient rearrangement and polar factorization, up to almost-everywhere equality. For 1D distributions, monotone/quantile coupling is selected by marginals; uniqueness within the chosen monotone or optimal-transport class does not imply that the real biological same-cell joint has this form. Known marginals cannot test whether the true coupling is the chosen optimum; discreteness or atoms require care about map and uniqueness. Population result; finite empirical transport accuracy is a separate question.

**Mixed Poisson primary source:** [Teicher 1961](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177705155) primary abstract states identifiability for mixtures of a one-parameter additively closed family, but available full text returned only an iframe; exact theorem and hypotheses could not be inspected. The earlier Feller 1943 result is cited in later literature, but its primary text was not located. The scalar Laplace derivation above remains independently stated, without attributing an uninspected theorem number.

**Anchor testability qualification:** [Freyaldenhoven et al., working paper, Theorem 1](https://joseluismontielolea.com/anchor_words_wp.pdf) shows that a column-stochastic matrix of known nonnegative rank K admits a separable factorization iff a specified linear-program feasible set is nonempty; the authors construct a statistical test under their observational model. Thus existence of *some* anchor factorization is testable under these assumptions, contrary to any blanket claim of untestability. That does not verify a nominated biological feature's actual exclusivity, cross-assay class correspondence, or applicability of the text-model assumptions to selected sequencing counts.

**Recent unpaired manifold alignment:** [2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.11460) was discovered and primary HTML accessed. Proposition 3.1 and its model assumptions are evaluated below; the preprint does not establish project recoverability.

## Further targeted inspection and corrections

**Timilsina et al. Theorem 1 exact alignment alternatives.** Besides full-column-rank A^(q), full-rank E[ccᵀ] and Assumption 1 (geometric differences of the content/private joint laws on all specified non-private subspaces and their scaled simplex stripes), either (a) content coordinates are independent and non-Gaussian, with no pair related in distribution by any scalar and no coordinate symmetric about zero; or (b) the support of c is the symmetric hyperrectangle ∏[-a_i,a_i], with the same no-scaled-match and non-symmetry conditions. Either yields common Θ rather than two unrelated Θ^(q). Theorem 3 requires at least d_C actual matched training pairs, and therefore is unavailable; the earlier nonnegative-dictionary description was erroneous and has been corrected. Theorem 1 is population and an optimizer statement; approximate count-model fits are not theorem reproductions. The true latent assumptions are not established by matching observed projected distributions. See [primary theorem and appendix B](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.19422).

**Khemakhem et al. 2020, iVAE Theorems 1–3**, [primary pp. 4–6](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v108/khemakhem20a/khemakhem20a.pdf): observations pair x with an observed auxiliary u; z|u is factorial exponential family, x=f(z)+independent additive noise. Theorem 1 assumes the noise characteristic function vanishes only on a null set, f injective, sufficient statistics differentiable almost everywhere and linearly independent on every positive-measure subset, and nk+1 auxiliary values whose nk natural-parameter differences form an invertible matrix. Identifiability is only up to an invertible linear transform of sufficient-statistic vectors (~A), not individual components. Theorems 2 (k≥2, twice differentiable statistics and second cross derivatives of f) and 3 (k=1, nonmonotone statistics and continuous partial derivatives of f) give block-permutation equivalence (~P) and, in stated cases, componentwise recovery. The noise model is additive continuous, not the project's Poisson count layer; a valid jointly observed u with sufficient variation is not established. Auxiliary rank and latent conditional independence cannot generally be verified directly; observable fit can falsify aspects, not prove them. Theorem 1 is population; estimator consistency requires the paper's separate assumptions and is not a general finite-sample guarantee. No replication in this update.

**Slawski and Sen 2024, Proposition 2 and Theorem 8**, [primary JMLR pp. 6–7, 13–15, Appendix B](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume25/22-0058/22-0058.pdf): Prop. 2 considers a noiseless *permuted same-sample* problem; strict cyclical monotonicity yields the unique assignment optimum, a different observation design. Theorem 8 considers independently sampled X_i and Y_j, X on an absolutely continuous μ in R^d, Y distributed as f*(X)+ε with independent Gaussian ε and f*=∇ψ, ψ λ-strongly convex and L-smooth, and ||f*(X)||≤B almost surely. Algorithm 1 has the displayed high-probability empirical squared-error bound once m exceeds a dimension/B-dependent threshold; the leading deconvolution term is proportional to 1/log m and additional n,m terms appear. The target is a fixed-direction gradient-of-convex regression map on support, not a general coupling or DAG; count noise, cross-assay common coordinates and monotone mechanism are unverified, and independent Gaussian noise and structural direction are not established from marginals. Consult the theorem for full constants and exact expression; no fitted evaluation.

**Syrota et al. 2026, Propositions 3.1, 4.1, 4.2, 4.4 and B.14**, [primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.11460): a compact connected smooth data manifold with positive smooth density and specified noise/projection conditions is represented by two coordinate maps that are diffeomorphisms onto the *same* manifold. Their latent metrics are pullbacks of the same data metric, with corresponding pushforward probability laws. Prop. 3.1 identifies the true coordinate change iff the data metric-measure space has no nonidentity measure-preserving isometry; otherwise alternatives are exactly compositions with those symmetries. Prop. 4.1 gives the unique population GW coupling under this symmetry-breaking condition; Prop. 4.2 uses the stronger distinguishable-distance-distribution condition for the LP. Prop. 4.4 gives exact finite-sample permutation recovery when both encoders encode the same n≥3 observations, a shuffled-paired design. Prop. B.14 gives independent-sample consistency for global empirical minimizers under the stated compactness and symmetry conditions, not a global-optimization guarantee or rate. For different modalities, common observation space, shared pullback metric, diffeomorphic representation and noise-free reconstruction are substantive unverified assumptions; separate counts with assay-specific coordinates/noise need not meet them. A fitted manifold's internal diagnostics cannot establish that it represents the true shared metric. This preprint is not a causal-feature graph theorem. Paired-sample theorem is not admissible training information here. No reproduction.

**Shu and Tan 2020, Section 2 and Eq. (2), Section 4.2**, [primary pp. 263–275](https://statweb.rutgers.edu/ztan/Publication/Shu-Tan-CJS-2020.pdf): in primary sample T=1 observe U and outcome, auxiliary T=0 observe U and X. For moment restriction E[Φ(X,U;θ₀)|T=1]=0, identification by borrowing requires X ⟂ T | U, support of U in primary contained in that of auxiliary (their A2 and A3), and a uniquely determined θ solution. Common-variable marginal laws may differ. Their estimation and asymptotic claims add regression/propensity and regularity assumptions; double robustness is about estimation model specification, not proof that selection ignorability is true. A causal IV interpretation additionally needs instrument relevance, exclusion and exogeneity and the intended structural equation. Shared instrument U is not verified available for these assays; selection ignorability, exclusion and exogeneity cannot generally be established from the separately observed distributions. ## OpenAlex citation-follow-up: additional primary results

**Shrestha and Fu 2025, Theorems 3.3 and 3.4**, [primary paper and appendices](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.03755): a single differentiable bijection g maps common-content c and domain-style s^(n) into the **same** observed R^d coordinate space in every domain, with open continuous supports and no explicit random count measurement. Assumption 3.1 postulates mutual block independence of content and styles; Assumption 3.2 requires that every positive-mass measurable set that is not B×S differ in probability for at least one pair of domain distributions. With known dimensions, a differentiable injective population solution of exact content distribution matching and content/style independence recovers the content and style blocks up to injective transformations (3.3). Theorem 3.4 permits unknown true dimensions only if specified content/style dimensions are at least the true sizes, all latent densities are positive and finite over C×S, and the exact expected l0 style-sparsity objective subject to the constraints is solved. It similarly identifies blocks, not individual components, joint causal structure or a genomic DAG. Their multi-domain GAN with sparsity regularization is a practical approximation; finite-sample consistency and successful nonconvex optimization do not follow from these identification statements. Count noise and assay-specific feature maps are not covered by the stated model, selection might break a common content law, and latent independence and domain-variability assumptions are not directly verified by goodness of fit. Primary paper Appendix B explicitly compares Sturma and Timilsina. This is a positive candidate for Astra's applicability decision, not an approved implementation or a replication.

**Xi et al. 2024, Proposition 3.1**, [primary NeurIPS paper pp. 3–4, Appendix C](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/ff56c8dc4c61017eab9771481b7fda0d-Paper-Conference.pdf): under their injective observation and treatment-invariance assumptions the modality-specific propensity functions coincide with the latent propensity and summarize treatment-relevant shared information. The score may be noninjective; it does not identify a biological full joint or causal graph. Treatment/perturbation labels are required for the learner, not authorized in the present data contract. Exclude this fitting route, while retaining it as a conditional theoretical reference.

**Ben-Ari et al. 2025, [primary paper](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/429b76977aa377ed6dff3b91e9163b40-Paper-Conference.pdf)**: experiments use a small number of genuine pairs for the CCA alignment step, including the reported near-unpaired setups (Section 4, Figure 5). This source does not meet the current no-paired-training contract; strong retrieval performance does not establish population causal identifiability.


**Shrestha–Fu Theorem 3.3 (known dimensions):** The model and Assumptions 3.1–3.2 above, exact distribution matching and block independence constraints, differentiable injective encoder and known content/style dimensions imply injective block transformations for both content and style. Full conditions are in Section 3.1 and proof in Appendix C. Population statement.

**Shrestha–Fu Theorem 3.4 (unknown dimensions):** In addition to the 3.3 conditions, assumed encoder dimensions each upper-bound true dimensions, latent density is strictly positive and finite on the common product support, and an exact expected ℓ0 minimizer satisfies the specified constraints; output blocks identify content/style up to injective block transformations. This does not permit selecting arbitrarily small dimensions. Section 3.2, proof Appendix D. Population statement.
